Basic parameters

A new drive knows nothing about the motor connected to it. The first thing you do: read it the motor nameplate, then tell it how fast to start, how fast to stop, and where to take its orders from. These parameters exist on every brand, under different names.

1Before you change any parameter

If you are not sure how the drive is set, do a factory reset first so you start from a known state. Then begin with the quick setup menu, because it holds all the basic parameters. And write down every parameter you change so you can go back to it when a fault comes.

2Step one: tell the drive about the motor

The drive must know the motor it is running, so you copy 5 numbers from the nameplate: the voltage (for the delta or star connection), the current for overload protection, the frequency, the speed so it can work out slip, and the power.

Every brand names these parameters differently, but the idea is the same. Here are 3 popular brands. Check the manual for your model:

3The ramp: how long to start and stop?

Accel is the time the motor takes from zero to maximum speed, and decel is the time from maximum speed to zero. So with accel at 10 s and a 50 Hz maximum, the motor reaches 25 Hz in 5 s.

Make the time too short and faults appear: a short accel draws high current and trips OC, and a short decel feeds energy into the DC bus and trips OV, as we saw in lesson 2.

Drill: work out the ramp time

4Speed limits and where commands come from

Next you set the speed limits. Min freq stops a pump running dry and a motor overheating at low speed, and max freq keeps you inside the motor and gearbox limits.

Last, you tell the drive where its commands come from: the start command from the keypad, the terminals or comms, and the speed from the pot, an AI, a preset or comms. The stop mode is ramp or coast, covered in lesson 8.

Motor won't turn? Check the start source and speed source first.
