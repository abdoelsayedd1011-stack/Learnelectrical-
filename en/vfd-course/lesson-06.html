Basic parameters

A new drive knows nothing about the motor connected to it. The first thing you do: read it the motor nameplate, then tell it how fast to start, how fast to stop, and where to take its orders from. These parameters exist on every brand, under different names.

1Before you start

  • If the drive is used, or you are not sure of its settings: restore the factory settings (factory reset) first.
  • Most drives have a quick setup or a «basic» group with only the important parameters. Start there.
  • Write down every parameter you change, or save it on a computer, in case the drive gets replaced.

2Motor nameplate → parameters

The 5 most important numbers: voltage and current (depending on how the motor is connected: delta or star), frequency, speed and power. From them the drive works out overload protection, slip and the autotune.

3The ramp: accel and decel

Accel time = the time from 0 to max speed (or to rated frequency on some brands). Decel time = the time from max speed to 0. The time is proportional to the change in frequency.

Example: accel = 10 s from 0 to 50 Hz. The motor reaches 25 Hz in 5 s. If you make the accel far too short on a heavy load ⇒ an OC fault. If you make the decel far too short ⇒ an OV fault.

Drill: work out the ramp time

4Speeds and command source

  • Min frequency: for pumps, so they never run with no flow, and for a self-cooled motor, so it does not overheat at low speed for long periods.
  • Max frequency: do not exceed the limits of the motor, gearbox and pump. 60 Hz on a 50 Hz motor is usually possible, but with less torque.
  • Run command source: keypad, terminals (2-wire / 3-wire), or communication (Modbus, EtherNet/IP, PROFINET…).
  • Speed source: keypad pot, AI1, AI2, preset speeds, or communication.
  • Stop mode: ramp or coast (lesson 8).
The most common reason a motor «won't turn»: the run command comes from one place and the drive expects it from another. The moment you suspect it, check the command source and speed source parameters.
