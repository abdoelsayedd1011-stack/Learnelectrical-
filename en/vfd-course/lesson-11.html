Safety and installation

A VFD runs on a high DC voltage, puts out fast pulses that make noise, and gets hot. This lesson is about how to work on it without getting hurt, and how to install it so it lives for years without strange faults.

1Before you touch

The golden rule: switch off and lock (LOTO), wait the time printed on the drive, and measure yourself. A dark CHARGE lamp or a dark display is no guarantee.
  • STO (Safe Torque Off) stops the motor from turning, but it does not disconnect the power. The terminals are still live.
  • A motor turned by its load (a fan in the wind, a pump with water flowing back through it) generates voltage at the drive terminals even with the power off. Lock the load mechanically.
  • Use a multimeter rated at least CAT III, on a V DC range that covers 1000 V.

3EMC and the motor cable

  • PWM pulses are very fast, so they make noise that reaches sensors, encoders and analog signals. The fix: a shielded motor cable, with the shield earthed 360° in the gland (not a twisted «pigtail»), and control cables kept apart from power cables.
  • A long motor cable (longer than the manual allows, usually tens of metres): leakage currents through the cable capacitance cause false OC and ground fault trips, and wave reflection produces voltage spikes at the motor that can reach twice the DC bus and damage its insulation. The fix: an output reactor, a dV/dt filter or a sine filter, and a lower carrier.
  • The built-in EMC filter: on isolated (IT) supplies, or with an earth leakage device, it usually has to be disconnected (with a screw or jumper). See the manual.

4Derating and mounting

A drive carries its rated current under certain conditions (temperature, altitude, carrier). If conditions are harder, you must reduce the allowed current or choose a bigger size.

FactorExample rule (from a typical catalogue)
Altitude1 % per 100 m above 1000 m
Panel temperatureAbout 2 % per degree above 40 °C (or above 50, depending on the model)
CarrierThe higher it goes above the default setting, the lower the allowed current
  • Mount it vertically, with space above and below for air (the manual says how many cm).
  • The panel needs a fan and filter that remove the heat: roughly 2–3 % of the drive power comes out as heat.

5Harmonics

The rectifier draws current from the supply in pulses rather than a sine, so it produces harmonics that heat transformers and cables and affect other equipment. The fixes: a line reactor or a DC choke (they reduce harmonics and protect the drive from supply spikes), and on large loads, harmonic filters or low-harmonic / AFE drives.

If there is an earth leakage device (RCD) before the drive, it must be a type suitable for drives (usually Type B for three phase) with a suitable sensitivity, or it will trip for no reason.
