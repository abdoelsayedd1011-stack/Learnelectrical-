Safety and installation

A VFD runs on a high DC voltage, puts out fast pulses that make noise, and gets hot. This lesson is about how to work on it without getting hurt, and how to install it so it lives for years without strange faults.

1Before you touch any terminal

LOTO → wait the marked time → measure yourself. A dark CHARGE lamp is no guarantee.

Three things can fool you. STO stops the motor turning, but the terminals are still live. And if the load turns the motor, the motor generates voltage even with the drive isolated, so lock it mechanically.

When you measure, use a CAT III multimeter on V DC that reads up to 1000 V.

3Noise and the motor cable

The fast pulses of a drive radiate noise that upsets sensors. The cure is shielded motor cable, with the shield earthed 360° and not by a pigtail, and control cables kept apart from power.

With a long motor cable you get false OC trips and voltage spikes at the motor. Then fit an output reactor or a dV/dt filter, or lower the carrier.

On an IT network or with an RCD, disconnect the internal EMC filter.

4Derating: when does the drive give less current?

A drive is cooled by air, so the harder the conditions, the more you must reduce the current or pick a bigger size. For altitude, take off 1 % per 100 m above 1000 m. For temperature, about 2 % per degree above 40 °C. A higher carrier reduces the current too.

For the cooling to work, mount the drive vertically with air gaps around it. The panel itself needs a fan, because 2–3 % of the power comes out as heat.

Drill: work out the derating

5Harmonics: what the drive does to the mains

The rectifier draws current from the mains in pulses, not as a sine wave. Those pulses create harmonics that heat transformers. The cure is a line reactor or a DC choke, and filters or an AFE for large loads.

RCD before the drive? It must be Type B, or it trips for no reason.
