Why a VFD?

On its own, a 3-phase motor runs at roughly one speed and pulls a huge current while it starts. A VFD (variable frequency drive, also called an inverter) solves both problems: it starts the motor gently and lets you choose its speed. In this lesson you compare starting methods yourself and see how a small cut in speed saves a lot of electricity.

1What goes wrong when a motor is put straight on the mains?

A motor connected directly to the mains (DOL) draws 6 to 8 times its nameplate current while it starts. That surge pulls the voltage down, so the lights flicker, and the sudden start hammers the coupling and gearbox.

Once it is running, the motor is stuck at one speed: on or off.

A VFD solves both problems. It takes the fixed 50 Hz mains and gives the motor variable frequency and voltage, so the motor starts smoothly and runs at the speed we choose.

2A VFD or another starting method?

Other methods also cut the starting current, such as star/delta and the soft starter. But a VFD starts the motor at about nameplate current only:

  • DOL
    6–8 ×
  • Star / delta
    ≈ 2–2.5 ×
  • Soft starter
    2.5–4 ×
  • VFD
    ≈ 1 ×

The VFD is also the only one that changes the speed after the motor has started. Try it on the same 15 kW motor: pick a method, press Start and compare the red current.

3How does a VFD change the speed?

Motor speed depends on two things: the frequency of its supply and its number of poles:

ns = 120 × f ÷ P

So a 4-pole motor on 50 Hz runs at 1500 rpm (the nameplate says about 1450 because of slip). If the VFD drops the frequency to 25 Hz, the speed drops to half: 750 rpm.

Because the VFD controls the frequency, it gives you more on top: a smooth ramp when starting and stopping, reversing without a contactor, protection against overload, short circuit and phase loss, and a readout of current and power.

4Why does it save energy on pumps and fans?

On pumps and fans, flow follows speed, pressure follows speed squared, and power follows speed cubed. So at 80 % speed the flow is 80 %, but the power falls to just 51 %:

  • Flow
    80 %
  • Pressure
    64 %
  • Power
    51 %

That is why, when you need less flow, you lower the speed with the VFD instead of closing a valve with the motor at full speed. Move the slider and watch the saving:

5An example from the field

A 55 kW pump ran 24 hours a day with its valve half closed. After a VFD was fitted and it ran at a lower speed, the VFD paid for itself from the energy saved in under a year.

But if the speed is fixed and all you need is a soft start, a soft starter is cheaper and does the job.
