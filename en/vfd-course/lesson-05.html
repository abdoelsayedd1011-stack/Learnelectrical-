Control terminals

The keypad is fine for testing, but in a plant the drive takes its orders from the terminals: a selector, a push button or a PLC to run, a pot or an analog signal for speed, and it answers through a relay that says «I'm running» or «I've tripped». You will wire all of this and run it yourself.

1Digital inputs (DI): how do you give a command?

The digital inputs are where you give the drive its commands. Each DI is programmed for one function: run, reverse, stop, a preset speed, or reset.

For a DI to see the command you need to know its type. PNP (source) turns on when it gets +24 V, and NPN (sink) turns on when it is connected to 0 V.

The run command itself has two styles. 2-wire is a maintained selector, and its drawback is that the motor restarts by itself when power returns. 3-wire is a momentary Start button plus a Stop button, which is safer.

For preset speeds you use 2 DIs together: 00 means speed from the analog input, and 01, 10 and 11 mean Preset 1, 2 and 3.

2The speed signal (AI): where does the drive get the speed?

The speed you want comes in on the analog input. The simplest way is a 10 kΩ pot wired to +10V, AI and 0V. If it works backwards, swap the two outer wires.

If the signal comes from a PLC it is 0–10 V or 4–20 mA. The first picks up noise. The second is better: it travels long distances and reveals a broken wire.

The drive converts the signal to a frequency in proportion, between the minimum and maximum frequency:

f = fmin + (signal − min) ÷ span × (fmax − fmin)

3Outputs: how does the drive answer back?

The drive also sends signals out. The relay output (NO/NC) is programmed to report a state: fault, run, ready, or at speed. The analog output sends the speed or current to a meter or a PLC.

There is also a safety input called STO: it cuts motor torque in hardware. If its jumper is missing, the drive will not run.

4Wire it and run it

Now put it all together: wire Run to DI1, direction to DI2, the pot to AI1, and the lamp to the relay.
