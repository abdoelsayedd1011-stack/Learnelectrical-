Control modes and autotune

The same drive can run the motor with more than one «brain». V/f is simple and suits fans, while vector understands the motor from the inside, so it gives far more torque and accuracy at low speed. Try both on a loaded conveyor and see the difference for yourself.

1Slip: why speed drops with load

An induction motor turns a little slower than synchronous speed, and this difference (slip) grows with load. A motor with 1500 rpm synchronous speed may turn at 1450 rpm at full load: that is 50 rpm of slip.

The problem at low speed: at 5 Hz the synchronous speed is only 150 rpm, and the slip is still about 50 rpm! That is a 33 % speed error. And V/f torque gets weak there too.

4Autotune

Vector needs to know the «inside» of the motor: winding resistance, inductance and magnetising current. The autotune measures them itself.

  • Enter the nameplate data first (lesson 6). Without it the autotune measures wrongly.
  • Static (the motor does not turn): when you cannot uncouple the load. Faster, and accurate enough for most jobs.
  • Rotating (the motor turns): more accurate, but the motor must be uncoupled from the load or lightly loaded, with no danger if it turns.
  • The motor is cold, the cable is in its final connection (its length is part of the measurement), and no output contactor is open.
Safety: in a rotating autotune the motor turns by itself. Make sure nobody is near the machine and the load is uncoupled, especially on a hoist.
