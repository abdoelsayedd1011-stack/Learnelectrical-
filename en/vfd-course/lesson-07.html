Control modes and autotune

The same drive can run the motor with more than one «brain». V/f is simple and suits fans, while vector understands the motor from the inside, so it gives far more torque and accuracy at low speed. Try both on a loaded conveyor and see the difference for yourself.

1Why does speed drop when the motor is loaded?

A loaded motor turns a little slower than the speed its frequency sets. That gap is called slip, and it is roughly constant: about 50 rpm.

At 50 Hz the gap hardly shows: 1500 becomes 1450, an error of just 3 %. But at 5 Hz the same 50 rpm takes the speed from 150 down to 100, an error of 33 %. That is why low speeds need smarter control.

23 control modes: which one do you pick?

V/f is the simplest mode and is enough for fans, pumps, and one drive feeding several motors. Its weakness is that torque fades below 5–10 Hz.

SVC (sensorless vector) gives high torque down to 1 Hz without an encoder. It suits conveyors and mixers.

FVC is vector control with an encoder on the motor, so it gives full torque even at zero speed. This is for hoists and winders.

4Autotune: the drive gets to know the motor

The vector modes need to know the motor from the inside, and autotune measures it. There are two kinds: static measures with the motor stopped and the load coupled, and rotating spins the motor so it is more accurate, but the load must be uncoupled.

Before you run it, check 4 things: nameplate data entered, motor cold, the final cable connected, and no open contactor on the output.

Rotating: the motor spins by itself. Clear people away and uncouple the load, especially on hoists.
