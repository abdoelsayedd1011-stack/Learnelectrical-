PID and applications

Most drives have a PID controller inside. That means you can wire a pressure transmitter straight to the drive, and it changes the pump speed by itself so the pressure stays constant however often people open and close taps. No PLC needed.

1The idea

  • SP (setpoint): the pressure you want, for example 4 bar, from the keypad or from a signal.
  • PV / feedback: the real pressure from a 4–20 mA transmitter on AI2 (range 0–10 bar, for example).
  • The output: pump speed. If the pressure is below the setpoint ⇒ higher speed, and the other way round.
SettingWhat it doesIf too large
Kp (proportional gain)Reacts in proportion to the errorOscillation
Ti (integral time)Removes the remaining error over time. The shorter it is, the strongerIf far too short: oscillation and overshoot
Td (derivative)Looks at how fast the error changesUsually left at zero on pumps

2Try it: a constant-pressure pump

The current settings are too «eager» and the pressure oscillates. Tune them, then change the demand and watch the drive bring the pressure back.

Constant-pressure pump with PID

A quick tuning method: start with a small Kp and a long Ti (slow but stable). Raise Kp until it starts to oscillate, then go back to about half. Then shorten Ti until the error goes away quickly without oscillation.

3Sleep and wake

At night there is no demand and the pump turns at low speed just to hold the pressure. The drive can stop it (sleep) when the speed stays below a limit for a set time, and wake it up (wake) when the pressure drops below a limit. Try it in the simulator: close all the taps (0 %) and wait.
