1. The idea

The machine forms a paper tube and fills it with product, then seals and cuts the packages. For every package to have the same volume, the product level inside the tube must stay constant all the time. That is the job of the TMCC2 A401 card.

Product tank→Regulating valve→Flowmeter→Paper tube→Packages

The card reads two things: flow from the flowmeter, and level from a sensor that reads the position of a floater inside the tube. It outputs one thing: a voltage to the I/P transducer that opens the valve with air. The PLC gives the card run and stop commands and receives a "ready" signal from it.

This guide applies to both A3/Flex 150 and A3/Flex 160: the filling card, its program and its PLC signals are the same on both.

The key point: the card controls the filling on its own. The PLC does not calculate anything for the filling; it only tells the card "run" or "stop" and watches the ready signal.

2. Parts and signals

PartWhat it does
TMCC2 card A401Runs the filling program FCSFA3F and calculates the valve opening continuously. It has a small 8-character display, menu buttons and an RS232 service port.
Flowmeter B1316Measures flow in l/h and sends the reading to the card over an RS485 link (not an analog wire).
Level sensor B1314Reads the floater position inside the tube, i.e. the product level in %.
I/P transducerTakes 0 to 10 V from the card and converts it to 0 to 4 bar of air.
Regulating valveOpens according to the air pressure from the transducer.
PLCGives the production and short stop commands and reads the card's signals.

Signals between the card and the PLC

They are plain digital wires, not a network:

%I269   Enabled        card ready, no fault
%I268   Filling OK     flow close to setpoint
%I266   High level     tube level high
%Q146   Production     production command from PLC to card
%Q148   Short stop     short stop command to card
If %I269 Enabled drops while the machine is running, the PLC counts 25 seconds; if it does not come back, the alarm TMCC Filling system not enabled appears. The alarm is not latched: it clears as soon as the signal returns.

3. Flow loop and level loop

The card runs two nested loops. The outer loop watches the level and decides the required flow; the inner loop moves the valve so the real flow reaches that setpoint.

Level setpoint L→Level loop U V W→Flow setpoint→Flow loop P I D→Valve

Nominal flow

The starting point is the nominal flow: package volume × machine speed.

Nominal flow = 0.250 L × 7000 pk/h = 1750 l/h

The level loop adds to or subtracts from this value depending on whether the level is above or below target.

Flow loop (P, I, D)

PHow strongly the valve reacts to the difference between flow setpoint and measured flow. Bigger = faster response; too big = valve oscillates.
IRemoves the small remaining difference over time. Without it the flow stays a bit above or below the setpoint.
DBrakes the movement when the difference changes quickly. Usually left at zero.

Level loop (U, V, W)

ULike P but for level: how much the level affects the flow setpoint. Big = level returns quickly but may swing.
VLike I for level: brings the level exactly back to the target over time.
WLike D for level. Usually zero.
LLevel setpoint in the tube, in %.
Simple rule: if the flow itself oscillates, look at P and I. If the flow is steady but the level slowly rises and falls, look at U and V.

4. Start-up and prefill

Starting with an empty tube

When production starts the tube is empty and the level loop cannot work properly yet, so the card starts with a fixed, calculated flow:

BStart flow as a percentage of nominal flow (e.g. 120% to fill the tube quickly).
TRamp time: flow rises from zero to B over this time so the valve does not open all at once.
GSwitch point (virtual level trigger): when the level reaches this value, the card leaves the start flow and hands control to the level loop.

Sequence: ramp to B, level rises, reaches G, switch to level loop, level settles at L.

If G is too low, the switch happens while the tube is still almost empty, the level loop opens the valve hard and the level overshoots. If G is too high and close to L, the level passes the target before the switch.

Prefill

After a short stop the tube is not completely empty. Instead of starting from scratch, the card does a "prefill": it fills the tube at a fixed flow (a percentage of nominal) until the level is back near target, then returns to the level loop. This gets back to production faster and without underfilled packages.

In the simulator the prefill percentage is called PF, and the switch from it happens at G + 10% or L, whichever is lower. These numbers are approximate, for teaching.

Example: changing the flow loop P

  1. Press any button: MONITOR appears.
  2. Arrow down once: SETTING; press ENTER.
  3. Arrow down once: FLOW PID; press ENTER.
  4. P CONST appears; press ENTER to see the current value.
  5. Change it with the arrows and press ENTER to save.
  6. Leave with BACK until you are back at the production display.

Empty pipe detection

By default detection is on with a 3000 Hz threshold. With that threshold the flowmeter reads products with a conductivity of 30 to 35 µS/cm and above. For water with lower conductivity, detection must be turned off; the flowmeter then reads down to 5 µS/cm.

8. Hyperterminal

Connect a laptop to the RS232 port on card A401 with the service cable and open HyperTerminal or PuTTY with these settings:

Baud rate      9600
Parity         None
Data bits      8
Stop bits      1
Flow control   None

Type the command and press Enter. Note: what you type is not echoed on the screen; that is normal.

Extra service commands:

FCLEAR          restore default flowmeter configuration
EPIPE ON|OFF    empty pipe detection on / off
EPIPETH    detection threshold (default 2300, max 3000 Hz)
On a real machine: for diagnosis use only S, Z and H. Never type STOP, FCLEAR or F during production: STOP stops the filling, FCLEAR erases the flowmeter setup, and F opens the valve without control.

Note: the manual gives 3000 Hz as the default in the card menu section and 2300 Hz in the terminal commands section. Check your machine's value before changing it.

9. Forcing

Forcing (menu FORCE › OUTPUT or command F) makes the card stop controlling: the valve stays at the percentage you set, nothing else. The card still reads flow and level but does not use them.

  • If the forced value is above what the machine consumes, the level rises until the high level signal and the tube floods.
  • If it is below, the level falls and packages are underfilled.
  • Filling OK may drop if the flow is further from setpoint than the WIND window.

Forcing is meant for testing the I/P transducer and valve during preparation, not during production. Always undo it after a test: F 0 or O=0.

The manual does not say whether the real card drops Enabled while forcing. The simulator assumes it does not (worst case): the PLC will not know the output is forced.

10. Fixing "Flowmeter Communication error"

This message appears on the card's own display and means the card cannot talk to the flowmeter over RS485. The card closes the valve and drops Enabled, and after 25 s the PLC shows "TMCC Filling system not enabled".

  1. Check that the flowmeter has power.
  2. Connect the terminal and type Z. If the flowmeter does not answer, the problem is the link or the flowmeter, not the filling settings.
  3. Check the RS485 cable between flowmeter and card and its terminals at both ends: tightness, corrosion, water.
  4. Open the flowmeter cover and check the internal ribbon cable and its connectors.
  5. If all of that is fine, try another flowmeter or another card (with the same program: TMCC2 cards are not interchangeable).
Real case: this message appeared on a machine, and when the flowmeter was opened there was green corrosion on the internal ribbon cable. Fix: replace the cable, clean the connectors, and seal the cover and gland so moisture cannot get in again.

11. Using the simulator

  1. Open the simulator and press "Play the tour" for a 16-scene walkthrough with an English narrator, or start playing straight away.
  2. Run buttons: production, normal stop, short stop, alarm reset, and simulation speed.
  3. Move P, I, D, U, V, W, L, B, T and G and watch the valve, level and trend change. The drawing stays visible at the top while you move the controls.
  4. Fault buttons: trigger any fault from the table above and watch the alarms in the PLC panel.
  5. Card A401: the ▲, ▼ and ENTER buttons work with the menu from section 7.
  6. Hyperterminal: the black panel under the PLC signals; type a command or use the quick buttons.
Useful experiments: type EPIPETH 500 and watch the card think the pipe is empty while it is full, then EPIPETH 3000. Cut the flowmeter link and type Z. Type STOP and count 25 s to the PLC alarm, then RUN.
