Wiring the servo

We will wire a servo drive from start to finish: power, motor, encoder, brake, braking resistor, the PLC and the safety circuit. And you will see for yourself why swapping U and W on a servo is nothing like on an induction motor.

1Power: L1 L2 L3

  • A small drive is usually 200–240 V single- or 3-phase, and a large one 400 V. Read the nameplate, and for single phase the manual tells you which two terminals to use.
  • Before it there is a suitable circuit breaker, a main contactor (it opens in an emergency) and sometimes a noise filter.
  • Many drives also have separate control power terminals (names like L1C and L2C, or 24 V) so the display and the encoder stay on when the main power is switched off.

2The motor: U V W and PE — the order is mandatory

On an induction motor, swapping two phases simply reverses the motor. On a servo this is not allowed. The drive sends current according to the encoder angle, so if the phases are swapped the torque comes out in the wrong direction: the motor shakes or runs away on its own until an alarm trips. Reversing the direction is done with a parameter in the drive.

  • U to U, V to V, W to W, and the PE earth to earth. Use the ready-made cable if there is one.
  • Do not put a contactor between the drive and the motor that opens while it is running (unless the manual says otherwise). And do not put power factor correction capacitors on the output.

3The encoder, the brake and the braking resistor

  • Encoder cable (CN2 or a similar name): shielded and twisted, the shield earthed 360° in the connector, and kept away from power cables. In a cable chain you need a high-flex cable.
  • The brake: 24 V DC from a power supply (preferably a separate one), through a relay driven by the drive's BRK output, with a diode or varistor across it to stop the spark when it switches off.
  • The braking resistor: a small drive has an internal resistor (there is a jumper between two terminals such as P+ and D). For heavy loads you remove the jumper, connect an external resistor on P+ and C, and set the parameter to its value and power.

4Control: CN1 and STO

  • CN1 (I/O): carries the signals between the PLC and the drive: Servo ON, Alarm reset, the pulse inputs, the limit switches, and the Ready, Alarm and In-position outputs. The names and pin numbers differ from brand to brand.
  • STO (Safe Torque Off): two inputs from the safety circuit (safety relay, E-stop, guard doors). When they open, the drive cuts the torque in hardware whatever the program says. The drive usually comes with a jumper connector for STO for testing, but on a real machine it must be wired to the safety circuit.

Wire the drive yourself

Safety: wire with the power off and CHARGE out; first run without load.
