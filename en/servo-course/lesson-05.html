Wiring the servo

We will wire a servo drive from start to finish: power, motor, encoder, brake, braking resistor, the PLC and the safety circuit. And you will see for yourself why swapping U and W on a servo is nothing like on an induction motor.

1Where does power enter the drive?

The mains goes in on L1 L2 L3, at either 200–240 V or 400 V, so read the drive nameplate first. Ahead of the drive we fit a breaker, a main contactor and a noise filter.

There are also terminals called L1C L2C (or 24V): this is the separate control supply, so the drive stays awake and shows the alarm even when main power is off.

2How is the motor connected?

The motor goes on U V W and PE, and here the order is mandatory: U to U, V to V, W to W. The maker's ready-made cable is best.

On a VFD, swapping two phases just reverses the motor. On a servo the motor shudders or runs away. If you want to reverse direction, do it with a parameter.

And no contactor or capacitors between the drive and the motor.

3Encoder, brake and braking resistor

The encoder cable plugs into CN2. It must be shielded with the shield earthed 360°, and kept away from power cables. In a cable chain it must be a high-flex type.

The brake gets 24 V DC through a relay driven by the BRK output, with a diode to protect the contact.

To fit an external braking resistor: remove the internal resistor jumper (P+/D), connect the external one to P+/C, and set its parameter.

4Control terminals: CN1 and STO

CN1 is the signal connector. Servo ON, reset, pulses and limits go in, and ready, alarm and in-position come out.

STO cuts motor torque in hardware for safety. The jumper on it is for testing only, not for permanent running.

Wire the drive yourself

Wire with power off and CHARGE dark; first run without load.
