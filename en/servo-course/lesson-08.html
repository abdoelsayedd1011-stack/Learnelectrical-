Tuning and gain setting

The same motor and the same drive can be smooth and fast or humming and shaking, and the whole difference is in the gain setting. You will try auto-tune, then tune by hand and watch the curve change in front of you.

1What does tuning mean?

The drive does not know whether the load on the motor is heavy or light. Tuning means telling it about the load and setting how hard it reacts (the gains), so motion is fast and free of vibration.

Inertia ratio JL/JM: how many times heavier the load is than the rotor. If this number is wrong, every gain is wrong. Above 10–30 you need a gearbox or a higher-inertia motor.

2Each symptom and what it means

There are 3 main gains. Kv is the speed loop gain: too high and the motor hums and whistles (this also happens when the inertia ratio is entered larger than the real one), too low and motion is slow and affected by the load.

Ti is the integral time: too small and a slow oscillation appears.

Kp is the position loop gain: too high and the axis passes the target and comes back (overshoot) with ringing, too low and the following error grows.

While watching the motion you will meet 3 words. Overshoot means it passed the target and came back. Settling is the time until it holds still and gives in-position. Sluggish means it arrives late with no overshoot.

3Try it: auto-tune, then by hand

The right order: make sure the mechanics are sound, then run auto-tune. Next raise Kv until it starts to hum and back off 20–30 %. Then raise Kp until the stop is quick. Finally set Ti, and use a notch filter if there is resonance.
