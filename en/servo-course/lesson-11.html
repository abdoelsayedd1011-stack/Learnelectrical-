Common faults

When a servo is unhappy it raises an alarm, and the alarm tells you where to look. You will learn the meaning of the most common alarms, cause an overvoltage yourself and fix it, and solve 11 cases from real plants.

1The alarm tells you where to look

Every brand has its own alarm codes (AL.xx, Err or A.xxx…), but the basic meanings are almost the same:

AlarmMeaningMost common causes
OverloadHigh current for a periodHeavy load, brake applied, binding mechanics, gain humming
OvercurrentVery high instantaneous currentShort on U V W, damaged cable, motor insulation
Position deviation excessiveThe following error passed the limitAcceleration too fast, not enough torque, jammed load, gain far too low
Encoder errorA problem with the encoder or its cableBroken cable, connector, noise, shield
OvervoltageThe DC bus is highEnergy returned during braking and not enough resistor
UndervoltageThe incoming supply is lowVoltage dip, weak phase, contactor
OverspeedSpeed above the limitWrong U V W, wrong gear, high command, overshoot
Battery / Absolute lostThe absolute encoder batteryWeak or disconnected battery
  1. Write down the code and look at the drive's alarm history.
  2. Look up the code in the manual: its causes and steps.
  3. Look at the monitor: load %, DC bus voltage, following error.
  4. Ask: what has changed? (maintenance, a new load, a cable, a parameter).

2Experiment: overvoltage when stopping

The DC bus while braking

The fix is either a suitable braking resistor (and setting its parameter), or slower braking, or a lighter load. On large machines there are also units that return the energy to the grid, or a DC bus shared between drives.
