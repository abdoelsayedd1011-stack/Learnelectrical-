Inside the servo motor

We will open the servo motor and look inside: windings, permanent magnets, an encoder on the back, and sometimes a brake. At the end you will read the nameplate like a technician and calculate the power from torque and speed.

1What kind of motor is a servo?

A servo motor is a PMSM: the stator is a 3-phase winding like any motor, but the rotor is a permanent magnet.

That makes it synchronous: the rotor turns exactly with the field, with no slip, and the drive must know the magnet angle at every moment. Its torque follows the current through a constant called Kt (N·m/A), so controlling current is controlling torque.

Because the rotor carries no current, the motor comes out small, cool and quick to respond.

Never run it straight off the mains! It needs a servo drive.

3What is the motor brake for?

Some servo motors have a brake, and its job is to hold the load while the motor is stopped, not to stop it. With no power the spring clamps it, and when it gets 24 V DC it releases.

That is why we need it on vertical axes: a Z axis, a hoist, a lift, so the load does not drop when power is lost. Stopping with it is for emergencies only, because every stop shortens its life.

The drive controls it from its BRK output: it releases the brake after servo ON and clamps it before servo OFF.

Before removing a vertical-axis motor: prop the load mechanically.

4How do you read a servo motor nameplate?

The nameplate has 3 groups. First, power and torque: RATED OUTPUT is the power you use to pick the drive, RATED TORQUE is the continuous torque, and MAX TORQUE is about 3 times rated and is for acceleration only.

Second, speed and current: RATED / MAX SPEED, and above rated speed the torque falls. RATED CURRENT is what you compare with the drive current.

Third, the options: ENCODER, whose type the drive must support, and BRAKE, usually 24 V DC.

Power, torque and speed are tied by one formula:

P = T × 2π × n ÷ 60

For example: 2.39 × 2π × 3000 ÷ 60 ≈ 751 W, so it is a 750 W motor.

A low-inertia motor is for fast moves, and medium/high inertia is for heavy loads.
