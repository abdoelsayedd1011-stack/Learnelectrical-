Inside the drive

The drive has two parts: a power circuit exactly like a VFD, and a control section with three nested loops that run thousands of times per second. In this lesson you will see both in an interactive diagram.

1The power circuit: exactly like a VFD

If you understand the VFD, you will find the power circuit of a servo drive is the same:

  1. Rectifier: converts the AC coming in on L1 L2 L3 to DC.
  2. DC bus: large capacitors charged to about 310 V DC in a 220 V drive (and about 540 V DC in a 400 V drive).
  3. Inverter (IGBT): 6 transistors chop the DC with PWM and output 3 phases on U V W.
  4. Braking resistor (regen): when the motor brakes it returns energy, and this energy raises the DC bus voltage. The drive switches in the resistor to burn it off as heat.
Safety: the capacitors stay charged for minutes after the power is switched off. Wait for the CHARGE lamp to go out (and the time written on the drive), and measure with a multimeter between P+ and N before you touch any terminal.

2The control: 3 nested loops

The real difference between a servo drive and a VFD is in the control: the drive has 3 nested (cascade) loops, and each loop gives the command to the one inside it:

LoopComparesOutputsTypical rate
Position (outer)Target × actual positionA speed commandTens of Hz
Speed (middle)Target × actual speedA torque (current) commandHundreds of Hz
Current (inner)Target × actual currentPWM voltage to the motorSeveral kHz
Analogy: you are driving a car and want to stop at a sign. Position loop: "50 metres left, so I drive at 40 km/h". Speed loop: "I am doing 30 and want 40, more throttle". Current loop: your foot, turning "more" into the right pedal press. Each one must be faster than the one outside it, otherwise the car shakes.

Inside the drive (tap the blocks)

Following error (the error during motion): while moving, the actual position always lags slightly behind the target, because the position loop needs an "error" to ask for speed. This difference is called the following error or position deviation, and if it grows beyond a set limit the drive raises an alarm. Feed-forward reduces it by sending the required speed straight away without waiting for the error.
