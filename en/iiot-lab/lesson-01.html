What Are the Industrial IoT and Industrial Networks?

Plant data crosses several networks before it reaches your phone screen. In this lesson we take in the full picture first, then work our way up the pyramid from the bottom, one lesson at a time.

1Why don't plant devices understand each other easily?

The Industrial IoT (IIoT) carries shop-floor data to the cloud, so you see it on your phone and know about a failure before it happens. But devices don't speak one language; each layer has its own protocols.

At the bottom, at the sensors and actuators, we find PROFIBUS, CAN, Modbus RTU and IO-Link: small, fast, deterministic messages. In the middle, between controllers, we find PROFINET, EtherNet/IP and Modbus TCP. At the top we find OPC UA and MQTT, which carry lots of data that is not urgent.

Going down: messages get smaller, faster and more deterministic. Going up: bigger and richer in meaning.

2What are the families of industrial networks?

There are three families. The first is fieldbus, running on RS-485 or CAN, with Modbus RTU, PROFIBUS and CANopen.

The second is industrial Ethernet, on RJ45 or M12 connectors, with PROFINET, EtherNet/IP and EtherCAT. The third is IoT protocols, running on any IP network, with MQTT, OPC UA and REST.

The first moves bits in registers; the last moves clear meaning, like: oven/temperature = 182.5.

3What does a maintenance technician need to know?

You will meet situations like these. A VFD shows F070: is it the cable, the address or the termination? An HMI can't see the PLC: is the problem the IP address or the mask?

Management wants the numbers on a phone: you need a gateway, then MQTT, then a dashboard. And a bearing fails every 3 months: measuring vibration and heat gives you an early warning.

That is why the course runs in this order: the network and addresses (lesson 2), then the protocols (3–10), then the gateway and dashboard (11–13), then diagnosis and the project (14).

4Which terms will you meet again?

Master / Slave: one device asks and another answers. Cyclic / Acyclic: exchange every cycle or on request. Latency / Jitter: how long a message takes and how much that varies.

Topology: the wiring shape, line, star, ring or tree. Termination: a resistor at both ends of the line, and missing it is the top fault. And Tag: a readable name like Oven_Temp instead of the number 40001.
