What Are the Industrial IoT and Industrial Networks?

Plant data crosses several networks before it reaches your phone screen. In this lesson we take in the full picture first, then work our way up the pyramid from the bottom, one lesson at a time.

1The plant talks, but in different languages

Every production line carries hundreds of signals: oven temperature, motor current, part count, door status. These signals travel over industrial networks from the sensor to the PLC, from the PLC to the operator screen, and on to the computer that calculates throughput. The Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) is the layer that takes this data from the plant floor to the cloud, so you can see it on your phone and be warned before a failure.

  • Bottom of the pyramid: very small messages repeated every few milliseconds that must arrive exactly on time (deterministic). This is the home of the fieldbuses: PROFIBUS, CAN, Modbus RTU and IO-Link.
  • Middle: Industrial Ethernet: PROFINET, EtherNet/IP and Modbus TCP. Faster and larger, linking the PLC to screens and computers.
  • Top of the pyramid: large volumes of data that are not time-critical: OPC UA and MQTT feeding databases, the cloud and dashboards.
The rule: the further down the pyramid you go, the smaller, faster and more deterministic the message; the further up, the larger, slower and richer in meaning.

The first family deals in bits and bytes held in registers; the second carries the same registers inside Ethernet frames; the third carries meaningful information such as line3/oven/temperature = 182.5.

3What does a maintenance technician need to know?

You will not be writing cloud software, but you will face situations like these every day:

  • A drive shows communication fault F070 and the line is down: is it the PROFIBUS cable, the address or the terminating resistor?
  • A new HMI cannot see the PLC: wrong IP address or wrong subnet mask?
  • Management wants part counts and downtime on their phones: what do you need, from the gateway to MQTT to the dashboard?
  • A motor bearing fails every three months: how do you capture vibration and temperature and raise an alarm before it breaks?

The course follows the same order: first the physical network and addressing (Lesson 2), then each protocol in turn with a simulator exercise (Lessons 3 to 10), then the gateway, the dashboard and predictive maintenance (Lessons 11 to 13), and finally a troubleshooting method and a complete production-line project (Lesson 14).

4Terms you will meet again

Master / Slave (or Client / Server)One device asks, another answers. In Modbus the PLC is the master and the drive is the slave.
Cyclic / AcyclicCyclic data repeats every cycle (motor status); acyclic data is exchanged on request (reading a parameter).
Latency / JitterThe time a message takes to arrive, and how much that time fluctuates. Precision motion needs jitter below 1 µs.
TopologyThe wiring layout: line, star, ring or tree.
TerminationA resistor at each end of a serial line that prevents signal reflections. Forgetting it is the most common fault on PROFIBUS, CAN and RS-485.
TagA name for a value instead of a register address: Oven_Temp instead of 40001.
