EtherNet/IP, AS-i, IO-Link and HART

Four technologies you will meet alongside the networks so far: Allen-Bradley's Ethernet, the yellow cable for pushbuttons, the smart sensor, and digital data riding on the current loop. At the end, one table brings every network together.

1EtherNet/IP and CIP

EtherNet/IP (here IP stands for Industrial Protocol, not Internet Protocol) is the Ethernet network of Allen-Bradley/Rockwell and the ODVA partners. On top of TCP/UDP it runs the same CIP protocol used by DeviceNet, so the objects and description files are shared.

  • Implicit messaging (I/O): cyclic data over UDP on port 2222, at an RPI (Requested Packet Interval) such as 10 ms. This is how a ControlLogix exchanges data with POINT I/O and a PowerFlex drive.
  • Explicit messaging: on-demand request/response over TCP 44818: reading a tag from another PLC, or a parameter from a device.
  • In Studio 5000 you add the device from its EDS file, or as a Generic Ethernet Module using the Assembly instance numbers from its manual.
  • I/O traffic is usually multicast, so switches must support IGMP snooping; otherwise the traffic floods every port.
  • The ring topology here is DLR (Device Level Ring), the counterpart of MRP.
Classic problem: communication drops as devices are added: the module has run out of connections or its CPU packet budget (pps). Monitor connection usage on the network module, and raise the RPI for non-critical devices.

2AS-Interface: the yellow cable

AS-i is a network for the simplest sensors and actuators (pushbuttons, proximity sensors, valves). A flat yellow two-wire cable carries data and 30 V power together; devices mount onto it with piercing contacts, with no cutting or soldering.

  • One master (a card in the PLC or a gateway to PROFIBUS/PROFINET) and up to 62 slaves (31 in the older version), each with 4 binary inputs and 4 binary outputs.
  • Cycle time is about 5 ms for the whole network.
  • The address, 1–31 (A/B in version 2.1), is written to the slave with a handheld addressing unit; a new slave has address 0.
  • It needs a dedicated AS-i power supply (with data decoupling), not an ordinary 24 V supply. A parallel black cable carries additional 24 V for actuators.
  • Length is 100 m without a repeater, and no termination is needed. Branch freely (tree topology).

3IO-Link: the smart sensor

IO-Link is not a bus network but a point-to-point link between an IO-Link master (a block with eight M12 ports) and a single sensor or actuator, over an ordinary three-wire sensor cable (L+, L− and C/Q) up to 20 m long. The master itself connects to the PLC over PROFINET, EtherNet/IP or EtherCAT.

  • The C/Q wire operates either as a standard switching output (SIO) or as a serial communication channel (COM1/2/3 at 4.8 / 38.4 / 230.4 kbit/s).
  • Process data is cyclic (the actual value with no analog conversion: a pressure of 4.37 bar as a number); service data covers parameters (setpoint, unit of measure, serial number); and events carry warnings (lens dirty, overtemperature).
  • An IODD file describes the sensor (the counterpart of GSD). Data storage in the master keeps the sensor's parameters and reloads them automatically on replacement: the technician swaps the sensor and sets nothing.
  • Diagnostics: the port LED on the master (green = communicating, red = short circuit or wrong sensor), and the master reports each port's status to the PLC.

4HART: digital over 4-20 mA

HART superimposes a small digital signal (FSK modulation at 1200/2200 Hz) on top of the 4-20 mA current itself without changing its average value. The current loop still carries the primary value (pressure), while HART carries alongside it: the tag, the unit, secondary values (temperature), diagnostics and remote reconfiguration.

  • It needs a loop resistance of at least 250 Ω for the digital voltage to appear; most input cards include it.
  • A handheld communicator, or a HART modem on a PC, connects across the transmitter terminals or anywhere in the loop.
  • Multidrop: several transmitters on one loop with addresses 1–15; the current is fixed at 4 mA and values are read digitally only (slow: 2–3 readings per second).
  • WirelessHART: a 2.4 GHz wireless mesh network for remote transmitters, with a gateway that aggregates them to Modbus TCP or OPC UA.
