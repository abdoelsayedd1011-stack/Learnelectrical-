EtherNet/IP, AS-i, IO-Link and HART

Four technologies you will meet alongside the networks so far: Allen-Bradley's Ethernet, the yellow cable for pushbuttons, the smart sensor, and digital data riding on the current loop. At the end, one table brings every network together.

1What is EtherNet/IP?

It is Allen-Bradley's Ethernet, and the IP in its name means Industrial Protocol. On top of it runs the same CIP protocol we saw in DeviceNet.

It has two kinds of messages. Implicit (I/O) on UDP 2222, cyclic with a time called RPI. And explicit on TCP 44818, request and reply when needed.

To define a device you use an EDS file, or a generic module with assembly numbers. Because the messages are multicast, the switch needs IGMP snooping. And the ring here is called DLR, the counterpart of MRP.

Connections drop when you add devices? You ran out of connections or pps, so raise the RPI for non-critical devices.

2What is AS-Interface?

It is the flat yellow cable: two wires carry data and 30 V together, and devices clamp on with pins that pierce the insulation.

It carries up to 62 slaves, each with 4 inputs and 4 outputs, on a 5 ms cycle. Its length is 100 m with no termination, in any tree shape.

A new slave comes with address 0, so write its address with a programming device. And the supply must be a special AS-i supply, not an ordinary 24 V one.

3What is IO-Link?

It is a point-to-point link over an ordinary sensor cable (3 wires, up to 20 m) between a smart sensor and a master with M12 ports.

The sensor sends three kinds of data. Process data: the value as a number, like 4.37 bar. Service data: the setpoint and the unit. And events: like "lens dirty".

Data storage: swap the sensor and its parameters come back by themselves. The sensor description is in the IODD file.

4What is HART?

It is a small digital FSK signal riding on top of the 4-20 mA current. So the analog value stays as it is, and with it come the tag, the unit and diagnostics.

It needs at least 250 Ω in the loop. The communicator connects anywhere on it. In multidrop mode 15 transmitters share two wires with the current fixed at 4 mA. And WirelessHART is a wireless version on 2.4 GHz through a gateway.
