RS-485 and Modbus RTU

The network you will meet most often, on old and new machines alike: a twisted pair and Modbus. Master this lesson and you can solve most "no communication" faults on a drive.

1Why RS-485?

RS-485 is an electrical spec where a bit is the voltage difference between wires A and B. Because noise hits both wires together, it cancels out in the subtraction.

That is why the line reaches 1200 m and carries 32 devices on one pair.

No response from a device? Swap A and B first: nothing gets damaged.

2How is it wired correctly?

Devices are wired in one line from each device to the next (daisy chain), with no star branches. A 120 Ω resistor goes at both ends of the line only.

There must be a common GND between devices, or the transceiver burns. The shield is earthed at the panel end only. And the bias resistors are usually at the master.

3What are the serial port settings?

These settings must match on every device on the line. Baud is the speed: 9600 · 19200 · 38400 · 115200. Data / Parity / Stop is the character format, most often 8 · Even · 1, and with None we use 2 stop bits.

The slave address is the only one that differs: a unique number for each device from 1 to 247, with 0 for broadcast.

PLC at 19200 and drive at 9600 ⇒ constant timeout or CRC errors.

4How is data organised in Modbus?

Data lives in four tables, and each table has function codes (FC) for reading and writing:

1200 mDistance
32DevicesOn one pair
Offset trap: register 40001 is sent on the wire as address 0, and 40108 as 107. If a value reads "off by one register", this is why.

5What does the frame look like on the wire?

Each request is a short frame made, in order, of: the slave address, the function code, the first register, the register count, and a CRC check.

010300 6B00 02CRC

Errors come in three forms. CRC error: noise, missing termination or baud too high. Exception (the function code comes back + 0x80): 01 unsupported function, 02 wrong address, 03 wrong value, 04 device fault. And timeout: check the address, then baud and parity, then A/B, then the cable.

6How do we read and control a drive?

Each piece of drive information has a register. In this example:

  • 40001Command · 0x0001 = run
  • 40002Frequency × 100 · 5000 = 50.00 Hz
  • 40011Actual frequency
  • 40012Current × 10 · 123 = 12.3 A
  • 40020Fault code

Register numbers come from the device manual, and watch the scaling (÷100).
