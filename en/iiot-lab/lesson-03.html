RS-485 and Modbus RTU

The network you will meet most often, on old and new machines alike: a twisted pair and Modbus. Master this lesson and you can solve most "no communication" faults on a drive.

1Why RS-485?

〰️An electrical spec: a bit = voltage difference between A and B, so noise cancels.

1200 mDistance
32DevicesOn one pair

No response? Swap A and B first: nothing gets damaged.

2Wiring it correctly

  • One lineDaisy chain · no star branches
  • 120 ΩAt both ends only
  • Common GNDOr the transceiver burns
  • ShieldEarthed at the panel end only
  • BiasUsually at the master

3Serial port settings

🟰They must match on every device on the line.

  • Baud9600 · 19200 · 38400 · 115200
  • Data / Parity / Stop8 · Even · 1 (None ⇒ 2 stop)
  • Slave address1–247 unique · 0 = broadcast
PLC at 19200, drive at 9600 ⇒ constant timeout or CRC errors.

4Registers and function codes

0xxxxCoilsBit · read/writeFC 01 · 05 · 15
1xxxxDiscrete inputsBit · readFC 02
3xxxxInput registers16-bit · readFC 04
4xxxxHolding registers16-bit · read/writeFC 03 · 06 · 16
Offset trap: 40001 is sent as 0, 40108 as 107. A value "off by one register" = this.

5The frame on the wire

010300 6B00 02CRC

Address · function · first register · count · CRC

  • CRC errorNoise · missing termination · baud too high
  • Exception (FC + 0x80)01 function · 02 address · 03 value · 04 device fault
  • TimeoutAddress · baud/parity · A/B · cable

6Example: reading and controlling a drive

  • 40001Command · 0x0001 = run
  • 40002Frequency × 100 · 5000 = 50.00 Hz
  • 40011Actual frequency
  • 40012Current × 10 · 123 = 12.3 A
  • 40020Fault code

Numbers come from the manual; watch the scaling (÷100).
