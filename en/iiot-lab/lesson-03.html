RS-485 and Modbus RTU

The network you will meet most often, on old and new machines alike: a twisted pair and Modbus. Master this lesson and you can solve most "no communication" faults on a drive.

1Why RS-485?

RS-485 is not a protocol but an electrical standard: bits are sent as a voltage difference between two wires, A and B. Because noise affects both wires equally, the difference between them survives intact; this is why RS-485 reaches 1200 meters and connects up to 32 devices (or more with repeaters) on a single pair. Modbus RTU, PROFIBUS DP and others run on top of this standard.

Watch the labels: some manufacturers call A "D−" and others call it "D+". If a device does not respond, swap A and B first; reversing them damages nothing.

2Wiring it correctly

  1. A single line (daisy chain): from each device to the next. No star branches; stubs cause reflections.
  2. A 120 Ω resistor at the two ends only: many devices have a DIP switch or jumper for termination. Exactly two on the whole network: the first device and the last.
  3. Common ground (GND/SC): a third wire tying the device references together; without it the voltage difference can exceed the permitted range (−7 to +12 V) and burn out the transceiver.
  4. Shield: grounded at one end only (the panel) to avoid ground loops.
  5. Bias resistors: usually in the master; they hold the line in a known state when no one is transmitting.

3Serial port settings

These settings must match on every device on the line, or no one will understand anyone:

Baud rate9600 · 19200 · 38400 · 115200Higher speed = shorter distance. 19200 is a balanced choice

ParityEven · Odd · NoneThe specification prefers Even. With None, usually 2 stop bits
Stop bits1 · 2
Slave address1 – 247Unique for each slave. 0 is broadcast (no reply)
The classic fault: the PLC at 19200 and the drive at 9600. The result: constant timeouts or intermittent CRC errors.

The offset trap: address 40001 in the manual is sent on the wire as 0, and 40108 is sent as 107. Some software asks for the conventional address, some for the offset. If you get a value that is "off by one register", this is why.

5The frame on the wire

A request to read two registers starting at address 40108 on slave 1:

010300 6B00 02B6 D4
AddressFunctionFirst registerCountCRC
  • CRC-16 is two bytes computed by the sender; if a single bit is corrupted in transit, the receiver silently discards the frame. Repeated CRC errors = noise, missing termination or an unsuitable baud rate.
  • The response: 01 03 04 00 2B 00 00 xx xx: address, function, byte count (4), then the two values, then the CRC.
  • Exception response: the function code + 0x80, then an error code: 01 illegal function, 02 illegal data address, 03 illegal data value, 04 device failure.
  • Timeout (no reply at all): wrong address, wrong baud rate/parity, A and B swapped, or the cable.

6Example: reading and controlling a drive

Most drives provide a "command register", a "frequency reference register" and "status registers". A typical example (numbers vary by manufacturer; check the manual):
