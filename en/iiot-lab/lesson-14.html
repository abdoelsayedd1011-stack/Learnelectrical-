1The troubleshooting method: bottom up

Do not guess. Work through the layers in order, with one question and one tool per layer:

LayerQuestionToolCommon faults
1 PhysicalIs there a signal on the wire?Port LEDs, cable tester, ohmmeterBroken cable, loose connector, missing termination, A/B swapped, poor grounding
2 Data linkDoes the switch see the device?Switch MAC table, LLDP, ip neighDuplex mismatch, loop without RSTP/MRP, wrong VLAN
3 NetworkIs the IP reachable?ping, arp -a, tracertDuplicate address, wrong mask, missing gateway, firewall
4 TransportIs the port open?telnet ip 502, nmap, WiresharkService stopped, port blocked, connections exhausted
7 ApplicationDoes the device give a correct response?Modbus client, PRONETA, Wireshark with a protocol filterWrong unit ID, register address off by one, missing GSD, mismatched device name, expired certificate
Half of all faults are at Layer 1. Before any software: the LEDs, then the connectors, then the termination, then the voltage on the bus.

2The troubleshooting toolkit

  • Wireshark on a mirror (SPAN) port or a TAP: filters modbus, pn_io, mqtt, opcua, tcp.analysis.retransmission. Frequent retransmissions = a physical problem or congestion.
  • PRONETA (Siemens, free): maps a PROFINET network and shows names, IP addresses and devices with no name assigned.
  • ProfiTrace / PROFIBUS tester: measures signal quality for each station and reveals missing termination instantly.
  • Oscilloscope: on RS-485 and CAN, edges must be clean; reflections = termination, noise = grounding or a cable run next to power.
  • MQTT Explorer and UaExpert: to see for yourself what the broker is publishing and what the OPC UA server exposes.
  • Device logs (the Diagnostic buffer in TIA, the Module log in Logix) before anything else: they often state the cause in plain text.

3Ten cases you will encounter

  1. Modbus RTU occasionally reads zeros: noise on RS-485, missing termination or excessive length. Check for 120 Ω termination at both ends only, and grounding at one end only.
  2. Exception 02 (Illegal data address): an off-by-one address (40001 = 0), or the register does not exist on this model.
  3. PROFIBUS: a station drops out when a large motor starts: electromagnetic interference; the cable runs next to a power cable or the shield is not bonded.
  4. PROFINET: a new device has no connection after replacement: it has not been assigned its device name, or the topology is configured and it is on a different port.
  5. EtherNet/IP: connections drop at random: multicast without IGMP snooping, or the adapter’s CPU is saturated; review the RPI.
  6. CAN: bus-off: one node with a mismatched bit rate, or missing termination; measure 60 Ω between H and L.
  7. ping succeeds but the protocol fails: the port is blocked by a firewall or the service is not enabled on the device (e.g. enabling PUT/GET on the S7).
  8. OPC UA: BadCertificateUntrusted: the client certificate is not trusted by the server; move it to the trusted folder.
  9. MQTT connects then immediately disconnects: a duplicate client ID, or a keep-alive that is too short for a slow network.
  10. The dashboard shows a frozen value: the gateway has stopped and the last message was retained; you missed the heartbeat.

4The complete line project

A filling line: a Siemens S7-1200 PLC controls PROFINET I/O, a conveyor on a Modbus RTU variable-frequency drive, an IO-Link pressure sensor and a CANopen servo motor. A Node-RED gateway reads from the PLC over OPC UA and publishes MQTT to the server, which draws the dashboard and calculates OEE.

In the simulator you will find the line running with three hidden faults at different layers. Your task:

  1. Locate each fault with the right tool at the right layer.
  2. Fix it with the correct action from the list.
  3. Confirm that the data is back on the dashboard.

And this is the day-to-day work of an industrial network engineer: you do not write all the code; you make the parts talk to each other, and you know where to look when they fall silent.
