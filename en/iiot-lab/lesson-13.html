Predictive Maintenance

The purpose of every network so far: knowing a bearing will fail before it does. Here you learn what to measure, and how to turn the numbers into a planned maintenance decision.

1Three kinds of maintenance

TypeWhenCost
ReactiveAfter the failureUnplanned downtime, rush-ordered parts, consequential damage
PreventiveEvery N hours or monthsReplacing parts that are still serviceable
PredictiveWhen the data shows the onset of degradationSensors + analysis, but the downtime is planned

Predictive maintenance does not replace preventive maintenance; it moves part of it from “by the calendar” to “by condition” (condition-based).

2What do you measure?

  • Vibration: the most important indicator for motors, pumps and fans. An accelerometer on the bearing housing; the RMS value in mm/s is compared with the ISO 10816/20816 limits (e.g. for a medium-sized motor: good < 2.8, acceptable < 7.1, unacceptable > 11). Spectral analysis (FFT) identifies the cause: 1× running speed = imbalance, 2× = misalignment, bearing frequencies = bearing damage.
  • Temperature: for bearings, motors and panels; a gradual rise above ambient beyond the usual margin = alarm. Thermal cameras for panels.
  • Motor current: from the variable-frequency drive itself (Modbus), with no extra sensor. Higher current at the same load = friction or a blockage.
  • Run-hour and cycle counters: for contactors, valves and cylinders; their life is measured in operations, not days.
  • Differential pressure across filters, oil quality, and ultrasound for compressed-air leaks.

3From measurement to decision

  1. Baseline: record the machine in healthy condition for two weeks: mean and standard deviation of each indicator for each operating state.
  2. Limits: warning at +2σ, alarm at +3σ or at the ISO limit. Always with delay and hysteresis (Lesson 12).
  3. Trend: not the value but its slope: vibration rising 0.1 mm/s per week reaches the limit in N weeks. That is remaining useful life (RUL) in its simplest form.
  4. Context: high vibration at maximum speed may well be normal; compare against the same operating state in the baseline.
  5. Work order: the alarm creates a maintenance work order in the CMMS with the data attached, and the technician closes it with the actual root cause, so the rules improve.
Machine learning (anomaly detection, fault classification) comes after you have a year of clean data with an accurate failure log. Until then, limits and trends solve 80% of cases.

4Example: the cooling pump

A 15 kW pump on a variable-frequency drive. Sensors: vibration on the motor bearing (IO-Link), bearing temperature, current from the drive (Modbus RTU), discharge pressure (4-20 mA).

  • Weeks 1–2: RMS 1.9 mm/s, temperature 48 °C, current 24 A at 50 Hz.
  • Week 6: RMS 3.1 and rising 0.2 per week, temperature 56, current 24.5. Pressure steady.
  • Analysis: vibration and temperature rising together with near-constant current → the bearing, not the hydraulics. FFT shows the BPFO frequency → the outer race.
  • Decision: order the bearing and replace it during the planned shutdown in 3 weeks, instead of a sudden failure on the night shift.
