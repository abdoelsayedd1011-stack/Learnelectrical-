Dashboards, Trends and Alarms

Data you have collected is worthless until someone sees it and acts on it. Here you learn storage, charting, alarms that do not get ignored, and the OEE figure every manager asks about.

1From message to chart

The message has reached the server. What next? Three layers:

  1. Time-series database: InfluxDB, TimescaleDB or Prometheus. Every record = (time, tag, value). Built for millions of points and queries such as “average over the last hour”.
  2. Dashboard: Grafana, Node-RED Dashboard or Ignition Perspective. Trend charts, gauges, status indicators, tables.
  3. Alerts: rules applied to the data that send a notification (email, Telegram, WhatsApp via an API, a horn on the plant floor).

Retention: one reading per second for a year = 31 million points per tag. The solution: keep raw data for a week, one-minute averages for a month, and hourly averages for years (downsampling).

2Good alarms and bad alarms

An alarm that fires too often gets ignored (alarm flooding). Simplified ISA-18.2 rules:

  • Threshold + delay: “temperature > 200 °C for 30 seconds”, not “temperature > 200”. The delay kills momentary spikes.
  • Hysteresis: raise at 200 and clear at 195; otherwise the alarm chatters dozens of times around the threshold.
  • Priorities: critical / high / low / advisory, each with its own channel: a critical alarm wakes the on-call engineer; an advisory is read in the morning.
  • Acknowledge: an active, unacknowledged alarm stays visible until a person acknowledges it; log who acknowledged it and when.
  • Suppression: during maintenance or when the line is stopped, do not send “low pressure” from twenty sensors; a single “line stopped” alarm is enough.

3OEE: the number a manager understands

OEE (Overall Equipment Effectiveness) = Availability × Performance × Quality, and it is calculated from just three signals that can be taken from the PLC:

FactorCalculationExample
Availability (A)Run time ÷ planned time7 h ÷ 8 h = 87.5%
Performance (P)(Parts produced × ideal cycle time) ÷ run time3000 × 7 s ÷ 25200 s = 83.3%
Quality (Q)Good parts ÷ parts produced2900 ÷ 3000 = 96.7%
OEEA × P × Q0.875 × 0.833 × 0.967 = 70.5%

Signals required: Running (a bit), a part counter and a reject counter. From the Running bit alone you can derive downtime reasons if you add a reason code that the operator selects on the HMI screen.

85% is considered world-class, and most plants start at 40–60%. The number itself is not what matters; what matters is where the minutes are lost: a Pareto chart of downtime reasons is the first thing any manager asks for.

4Designing a dashboard that does not confuse

  • One screen per line: status (green/red), today’s OEE, parts vs. target, last alarm. Details go on a secondary page.
  • Plot the temperature trend with the upper and lower limit lines drawn; otherwise the curve is meaningless.
  • Put the unit next to every number, and show the timestamp of the last update; a dashboard showing a stale value is more dangerous than an empty one.
  • One colour for danger (red), and never use it for decoration.
  • A “gateway connected” indicator driven by a heartbeat message; if it stops, all values are greyed out.
