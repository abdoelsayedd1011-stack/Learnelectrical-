PROFIBUS DP

The purple cable found in almost every Siemens panel. Nearly all of its faults are physical: termination, addressing, connectors. Learn to read BF and SF and know exactly where to look.

1What is PROFIBUS DP?

PROFIBUS DP (Decentralized Periphery) is a fieldbus from Siemens and the PI partners, running over RS-485 on the familiar purple cable. A single master (the PLC) polls its slaves (I/O modules, drives, valve islands) cyclically: in every cycle it sends their outputs and receives their inputs. Up to 126 addresses, at speeds from 9.6 kbit/s to 12 Mbit/s.

The most common speed in plants is 1.5 Mbit/s. Modern slaves detect the baud rate automatically; older ones may need it set manually.

2Connector, cable and termination

  • The D-Sub 9 connector has two cable entries, IN (A1/B1) and OUT (A2/B2), plus a termination switch. With the switch ON, the OUT side is disconnected automatically, so the terminated connector must genuinely be at the end of the line.
  • PROFIBUS termination is not a simple 120 Ω resistor but a network (220 Ω between A and B, 390 Ω to +5 V and 390 Ω to GND), so it needs 5 V supplied by the device: power down the end device and the termination is lost, taking the whole network down.
  • Cable: Type A, twisted pair, green = A, red = B. Do not strip more than necessary and avoid sharp bends.
  • Limit: 32 stations per segment; more stations or a longer run requires a repeater or a fiber-optic OLM.

3Addressing and the GSD file

Each slave has an address from 0 to 125, set with DIP switches, a rotary switch or in software. The master needs a GSD file (plain text) for each slave type, describing its modules and data. Download it from the manufacturer's website and import it into TIA Portal or STEP 7, then drag the slave onto the network and give it the same address that is set on the device.

Common mistake: the switches say 5, the project says 4. Result: BF on the master, while the slave sits there with green LEDs and no data. The second classic: forgetting that some devices read their address switches only at power-up, so you changed the address without power-cycling the device.

4LEDs and diagnostics

LEDStateMeaning
BF (Bus Fault)Steady redNo communication at all: cable, termination, baud rate, or the master sees no slave
BFFlashing redAt least one slave is missing or faulty; the rest are running
SF (System Fault)RedConfiguration error or a diagnostic from a slave (missing module, undervoltage)
DIA on the slaveRedThe slave is reporting a diagnostic: read it from the master

In TIA Portal, Online → Accessible devices or the diagnostics view shows every slave and its status. On the cable itself, a tool such as ProfiTrace shows the signal waveform, reflections and intermittent stations. Without a tool, check: termination at both ends only, shield grounded, no stubs, and a length appropriate to the baud rate.

5Reasoning through a PROFIBUS fault

  1. Whole network fails suddenly: look for an end device that has powered down (termination supply lost), a connector that has come off, or a cable crushed under wheels.
  2. One slave missing: check its address, its power, its connector, and whether it sits beyond a break in the line (in which case everything after it is missing too).
  3. Intermittent faults: noise from an adjacent power cable, too much or too little termination, excessive length for the baud rate, or a stub. Lower the baud rate temporarily as a diagnostic: if the faults disappear, the problem is physical.
  4. After replacing a slave: set the same address, power-cycle it, and confirm that the GSD version matches the new device.
