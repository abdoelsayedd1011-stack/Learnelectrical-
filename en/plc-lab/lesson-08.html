Analog signals in the PLC

A digital sensor says "there is" or "there isn't". But a level transmitter says "the tank holds 2.35 metres". That number reaches the PLC as a 4–20mA current, the card converts it to a raw number, and you must convert that to the real value before you use it. This is called scaling.

1From the tank to a number

  1. The transmitter measures (for example a level of 0–4 metres) and outputs a current: 4mA = empty, 20mA = full.
  2. The AI card has an ADC that converts the current to a number. In Siemens, for example: 4mA → 0, and 20mA → 27648.
  3. The program converts the number to metres or % (scaling) and uses it in compares and alarms.

If you don't remember why we start at 4 and not zero, go back to the 4–20mA signal lesson in the Instrumentation course.

Value = Lo + Raw ÷ 27648 × (Hi − Lo)

3In the program: scale and compare

Each brand has its own instruction: NORM_X + SCALE_X in TIA Portal, SCP in Allen-Bradley, or you write the formula yourself with math instructions. The simulator has a Scale block that takes IW64 and puts the value in MW10 in the range you set.

Hysteresis: if the pump starts below 20% and stops above 20%, it will switch off and on every second and burn the contactor. The right way: start at 20% and stop at 80%, done with Set at the low limit and Reset at the high limit.

4Project: a tank with alarms

A level transmitter on IW64, a fill pump Q0.0, and the tank drains by itself (consumption). Write the program, press RUN and let the tank run by itself. Also try the "Cut the transmitter wire" button and think: what should the program do?

Simulator: the tank

On site: when the transmitter wire breaks, the card gives a wrong value, the scale says "0%", so the program starts the pump and fills the tank until it overflows! Real programs use the card's diagnostic bit or compare the raw value with sensible limits, and on a fault they stop the pump and raise an alarm. A hard-wired High-High float switch is the last line of protection.

5Common faults on site

Common faults on site:
  • The reading is stuck at a strange value (32767 or negative): a broken wire, or the transmitter has no power.
  • The reading is wrong by a constant ratio: the scale range in the program differs from the transmitter range (the program says 0–10 bar and the transmitter is 0–16).
  • The card is set to 0–10V and the transmitter is 4–20mA: the channel type must be set in the hardware configuration (and sometimes with a jumper on the card).
  • The reading jitters: an unshielded cable, or one next to motor cables. Separate them, and add a filter in the card or in the program.
