▶ The lesson in 5 minutes

Illustrated explanation, the simulator task solved, and the quiz answered. Made for the phone.

1The approach: from the outside in

  1. The CPU: is it in RUN? Is the ERROR or FORCE LED on?
  2. Input LEDs: when you press the button or the sensor operates, does the I0.x LED on the card light? If not → the fault is in the wire, the sensor or the power, not in the program.
  3. The program online: open the rung and see where the power (green) stops. That contact is the missing condition.
  4. Output LEDs: the Q0.x LED is on but the contactor is not running? → the fault is after the card: a fuse, a wire, a coil.
Online monitoring: what you see in the simulator in RUN (the green lines and the values inside the timers) is exactly what you see when you open the program "online" on a real PLC. It is the most important troubleshooting tool there is.

2Forcing

A force lets you hold an input or output at 1 or 0 whatever the real state is. On an input: test the program as if the sensor were active. On an output: test the contactor and the wiring without the program.

Danger: forcing an output really switches the device on, even when the interlock in the program says no. Forcing an input can make the program "think" a guard door is closed when it is open. Use it only when you are sure nobody is near the machine, and remove it as soon as you finish. A forgotten force = a mystery fault a month later.

In the simulator below: press Force, then tap any I or Q LED on the PLC bar: once for Force 1, again for Force 0, and a third time to remove it. Try Force 1 on Q0.0 and see the motor run without the program switching it on.

4Common faults on site

Common faults on site:
  • The FORCE LED is on at the CPU: someone forgot a force. Check the force table before you look anywhere else.
  • The program on the laptop is not the one in the PLC: someone edited online and did not save. Upload or compare before any change, and keep a dated copy.
  • The machine keeps stopping at the same point: a sensor at the edge of its range, or a wire that opens with vibration. A trace or trend on the input reveals it.
  • After replacing a card: the settings (analog channel type, address) in the hardware configuration do not match the old card.
