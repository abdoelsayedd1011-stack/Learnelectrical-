PLC course

A PLC is the industrial computer that runs machines instead of relay circuits. In this course you will learn how it works, wire sensors and contactors to its cards, and write Ladder programs yourself on a real simulator with buttons, sensors and moving machines: a motor with seal-in, star-delta with a timer, a box counter, a tank with alarms, a traffic light and a garage door. The simulator tests every exercise for you.

Before you start: you will get more from this course if you understand contactor control circuits (seal-in, interlock, star-delta) from the Classic Control course →, and sensors and the 4–20mA signal from the Instrumentation course →. It is not required, but it makes things much easier.

How to study here

  1. Read the explanation: one idea per part, in simple words
  2. Write the program on the simulator: tap a cell and choose a contact or a coil
  3. Press RUN and press the buttons: watch the power flow through the rung and the machine move
  4. Test the program: the simulator checks it for you. Answer the questions to mark the lesson done
Important: this simulator is a generic trainer with IEC / Siemens style addresses (I0.0, Q0.0, M0.0, T0 and C0). The idea and the logic are the same in every PLC, but address formats and instruction names differ: Allen-Bradley writes I:0/0 and O:0/0, while Delta and Mitsubishi write X0 and Y0. Lesson 3 has a comparison table, and below you will find simulators in each brand's style.
