What is a PLC?

A PLC (Programmable Logic Controller) is a small, rugged computer for factories: it handles heat, dust and electrical noise, reads buttons and sensors, and runs motors and valves according to a program you write. Here you will see why factories left relay circuits behind, and how a PLC "thinks" in a cycle called the scan.

1From relays to the PLC

In the past, every machine was controlled by relay circuits, contactors and timers wired together. That circuit was the "program". To change something small (a 5-second delay, or one more condition) you had to unwire, rewire and add relays.

Relay panel (relay logic)
  • The program = the wiring itself
  • Every change = manual work and material
  • Every timer and auxiliary relay = a part in the panel that can fail
  • Troubleshooting with a multimeter, wire by wire
PLC
  • Buttons and sensors are wired to the inputs, contactors to the outputs, and that is it
  • All the logic is a program: change it from a laptop in minutes
  • Hundreds of timers, counters and auxiliary relays inside the program, without buying a single part
  • Watch the program while it runs (online) and find where the fault is
Example: a filling machine had 30 relays and 8 timers. After the PLC: the same buttons, sensors and contactors, but all the auxiliary relays and timers became lines in the program. The panel became smaller and easier to maintain.
Note: a PLC does not replace contactors, overloads or breakers. The contactor still carries the motor current; the PLC only switches its coil. And emergency stop (E-Stop) circuits must still disconnect through hard wiring or a safety relay, not through the normal program alone.

2The scan cycle

A PLC does not watch the buttons "all the time" like a wire does. It runs 3 steps in a row and repeats them forever. Each round is called a scan:

  1. Read inputs: it takes a snapshot of all terminals at once and stores it in the input image.
  2. Execute: it runs through the program from start to end and calculates the results.
  3. Write outputs: it sends the results to the output cards all at once.

A scan usually takes 1–20 milliseconds, depending on the program length. Try it yourself in slow motion:

The scan cycle in slow motion

Remember: the program works on the image taken at the start of the scan, and the outputs only change at the end. If a signal comes and goes in less than one scan, the PLC may never see it.

5Common faults on site

Common faults on site:
  • The PLC is in STOP after someone downloaded a program or after a fault: all outputs are off. Check the RUN/STOP LED before anything else.
  • The ERROR / SF LED is on: a card is not seated correctly, a card is missing compared with the configuration in the program, or there is a power fault.
  • The 24V supply has dropped: a short in one sensor wire pulls down the whole supply, so all inputs go off at once.
  • The memory battery is empty on older models: the program or the values are lost when the power goes off. Always keep a copy of the program.
