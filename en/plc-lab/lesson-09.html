1Before you write: think in steps

Every machine runs in steps (a sequence): fill → mix → drain. The easiest way to write it:

  1. Write down the steps and what moves you from one step to the next (a sensor, a timer, a button).
  2. Make each step a bit (an M bit or the Q itself): Set it when its entry condition is true, and Reset the previous step in the same rung.
  3. The outputs are driven from the step bits.
  4. The Stop button resets all the steps.
The simulator here has 5 contacts per rung instead of 4, because the projects are longer. Press "Hint" if you get stuck, or "Show solution" after your first try to compare.

3Position control with an encoder

A question people ask a lot: "How do I make a machine stop at an exact position?" The answer: an encoder on something that turns with the movement, and the PLC counts its pulses to know how far it has moved. Take a cut-to-length sheet metal machine as an example:

StepCalculation
A 1000 pulses per revolution (PPR) encoder on a measuring wheel with a 200 mm circumference1000 pulses per 200 mm
The high-speed counter counts the rising and falling edges of both signals A and B (×4)1000 × 4 = 4000 counts per revolution
How much per mm4000 ÷ 200 = 20 counts per mm
A 500 mm sheet500 × 20 = 10000 counts
Why a high-speed counter (HSC)? The sheet moves at 150 mm per second, which is 3000 pulses per second. A normal input is read once per scan (every few milliseconds), so it would miss many pulses. The HSC counts in hardware between scans, and the program only reads the number and compares it.

The cycle: Start resets the counter → the motor runs fast → at 9000 (450 mm) it switches to slow → a little before 10000 it switches off and the brake holds → wait until the sheet stops completely → the knife comes down on a timer → when it is back up, reset the counter and count one piece → and the cycle repeats by itself until Stop.

Overshoot: even after the motor switches off and the brake holds, the sheet keeps moving a little, for example 50 counts = 2.5 mm. If you stop at exactly 10000, the sheet comes out 502.5 mm. The fix: stop early by the overshoot, that is at 10000 − 50 = 9950. That is also why we slow down first: the overshoot from slow speed is small and constant, but from high speed it is large and variable.
Homing and the Z pulse: a normal (incremental) encoder does not know where it is after a power loss; it only counts from the last reset. On machines that need a fixed position (such as an axis that moves back and forth) you do homing: move slowly to a Home sensor, then reset the counter on the Z pulse (one pulse per revolution) so zero is in the same place every time. Cut-to-length does not need it: each piece starts from the previous cut.

4Common faults on site

Common faults in projects:
  • The machine stopped in the middle of a step: the sensor that moves it to the next step is not working (a limit switch knocked out of place, a stuck float). Open the monitor and see which step is active and which condition is missing.
  • The pusher pushes the wrong box: the flag is cleared too early or too late, or the gap between boxes is shorter than the distance between the two sensors.
  • The door hits the end: the limit switch has moved or is not wired NC, and the motor keeps running until the overload trips.
  • Sheets come out longer or shorter for no reason: the encoder wheel slips on the sheet (oil, or weak spring pressure) or is worn so its circumference has changed. Clean it, measure a piece with a tape and adjust the counts per mm.
  • The safety photo-eye does not work: a dirty or misaligned lens. Test the safety photo-eye regularly: it protects people.
