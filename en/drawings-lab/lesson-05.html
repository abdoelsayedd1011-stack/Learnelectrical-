Single line and power circuits

The single line is the power map of the plant: from the transformer to the last motor. In this lesson you will read every number on it, follow the power path to a particular motor, and learn the difference between a single line and a power circuit, which draws each phase separately.

‹ LessonsAll courses →

1What is a single line?

Instead of drawing 3 phases, a neutral and an earth (5 lines), we draw one line and mark on it how many conductors it stands for. The point is to see the whole system on one page.

  • A thick line = a busbar in a panel.
  • Slashes /// on the line = the number of conductors (3 phases).
  • Next to each device is its rating: amps, kA, kVA, kW and the cable size.
  • Top to bottom = the direction of power: the source at the top and the loads at the bottom.

Example: a 1000 kVA transformer at 400 V ⇒ 1000 ÷ (1.732 × 0.4) ≈ 1443 A. That is why the 1600 A main breaker is set to 0.9 (1440 A). The short-circuit current is about 1443 ÷ 0.06 ≈ 24 kA, so the breaker's Icu must be higher than that.
Quick rule for motors at 400V: about 2 A per kW. A 37 kW motor ≈ 70 A. (The real figure is on the motor nameplate.)

4The power circuit and how to read it

The power circuit draws each phase separately for the same feeder. From top to bottom: L1 L2 L3 → motor protection breaker -Q1 (1-2, 3-4, 5-6) → the main contacts of -K1 → thermal element -F2 → terminals -X1 U V W PE → cable -W1 → motor -M1.

The same feeder, two ways

  • The dashed line across the three poles = a mechanical link: the poles open and close together.
  • Next to -K1 is a reference to the coil page in the control circuit, and next to -F2 a reference to contact 95-96.
  • On the cable: 4G2.5 = 4 cores, one of them green/yellow (G), each 2.5 mm².

Calculate and read

Safety: the single line shows you all the sources: if the panel is fed from two sources (a transformer and a generator, or feeds from two boards), you must isolate both. Only the single line shows this.
