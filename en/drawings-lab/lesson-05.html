Single line and power circuits

The single line is the power map of the plant: from the transformer to the last motor. In this lesson you will read every number on it, follow the power path to a particular motor, and learn the difference between a single line and a power circuit, which draws each phase separately.

1What is a single line diagram?

A single line draws one line instead of 5 wires, so you see the distribution of the whole plant on one page.

It reads from top to bottom: the source at the top and the loads at the bottom. A thick line is a busbar, the slashes on a line count the conductors, and next to each device its rating and cable size are written.

3What do the numbers on the drawing mean?

Transformer: 1000 kVA · 11/0.4 kV is its power and the two voltages it converts between.

Breaker: ACB 1600 A · 4P is an air circuit breaker with a 1600 A frame and 4 poles. Ir 0.9 means it trips on overload at 0.9 of its current. Icu 50 kA is the largest short-circuit current it can break.

Cable: 4C×185 Cu/XLPE/SWA is 4 cores of 185 mm² copper with XLPE insulation and armour. Motor: 7.5 kW · DOL is its power and starting method.

The current of any transformer comes from its power and voltage:

I = S ÷ (√3 × V)

So a 1000 kVA transformer at 400 V carries about 1443 A, which is why the 1600 A breaker is set to 0.9. Its short-circuit current (uk 6 %) is about 24 kA, so the breaker Icu must be higher.

A motor on 400 V draws roughly 2 A per kW: 37 kW is about 70 A.

4The power circuit: the same feeder in detail

The power circuit draws all 3 phases, in this order: L1 L2 L3, then the motor breaker -Q1, the contactor -K1, the overload -F2, the outgoing terminals -X1 (U V W PE), the cable -W1, down to the motor -M1.

The same feeder, two ways

It carries 3 important marks. A dashed line between poles means they move together. A reference such as /3.4 takes you to the page of the coil and 95-96. A cable name such as 4G2.5 means 4 cores including earth, 2.5 mm² each.
