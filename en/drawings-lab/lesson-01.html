Types of electrical drawings

The drawing set for a machine or a panel can run to 80 pages. You will not read them all: each type of drawing answers one question. In this lesson you will learn the main types, tell them apart at a glance, and know which one to open when you are standing in front of a fault.

1Which drawing do you open?

The drawing set of any machine holds many types, and each type answers one question. So before you open the file, ask yourself: what do I want to know?

To know where the power comes from and at what size, open the single line. To know how the motor is fed, open the power circuit. To know how the machine starts and stops, open the control circuit.

If the question is "which terminal does this wire go to?", it is the wiring / terminal drawing. "Where is the device mounted?" is the panel layout. "Which tank is the sensor on?" is the P&ID. "What type and length is the cable?" is the cable schedule.

3How do you recognise a drawing at first glance?

Each drawing has a distinct look. The single line has thick lines with kVA and kA. The power circuit has 3 lines L1 L2 L3. The control circuit has coils and buttons between L and N.

The wiring drawing has boxes with terminal numbers such as 13 and 14. The terminal drawing is a strip of squares numbered X1:1 upward. The P&ID has tanks, pipes and letters such as LT.

Every device in IEC drawings has an address in 3 parts: =A1 the function (the filling line, say), +CP1 the location (the control panel), and -K1 the device itself (the contactor).

The file is always ordered like this: index, symbols, single line, power, control, PLC I/O, terminals, panel, and finally BOM and cables.

The title block at the bottom of each page gives the drawing name, page number, revision and date.

5Mistakes made on site

The first mistake is working from an old drawing: check the revision and ask for the as-built copy. The second is looking in the wrong drawing, like searching for the coil in the single line when it is in the control circuit. The third is skipping the symbols page, although the first two pages save you hours.

The drawing shows what should be, not what is. Lock out (LOTO) and measure yourself before you touch.
