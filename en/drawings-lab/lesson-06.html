Reading drawings · Lesson 6 of 7

Panel layout and terminal strips

When you open the panel door you will find hundreds of wires. Two drawings let you find your way as if you already knew the panel: the panel layout (where each device is) and the terminal diagram (where each wire comes from and goes to). At the end you will trace signals from the field to the PLC yourself.

1What does the panel drawing show?

The panel drawing shows the mounting plate from the front, to scale, with each device carrying its tag, so you know where it is before you open the panel.

Mounting plate =A1 +CP1

The order inside the panel has a logic. From top to bottom: the isolator, then protection, then contactors, and last the terminals. Power devices are kept apart from the PLC and sensors. Devices that run hot, such as the VFD and power supply, go at the top.

2What are terminals -X1 and -X2 for?

The terminal is the border between the panel and the field: any cable from outside lands on it first. Usually -X1 is power and -X2 is control, so -X2:6 is terminal 6 on the control strip.

There are special kinds: a bridge links neighbouring terminals to the same node, the PE terminal is bonded to the DIN rail, and a fused or knife terminal opens for testing.

So any signal takes this route: the field device, then the cable and core, then the terminal, then the wire inside the panel, to the device or the PLC.

3Cable cores and ferrules

Core colours have a meaning. In power cables (IEC 60445): brown, black and grey are phases, blue is neutral, and green × yellow is earth. In an M12 sensor cable: brown BN is +24 V, blue BU is 0 V, black BK is the signal, and white WH is the second signal.

Multi-core cables such as 7G1.5 have cores numbered 1, 2, 3, and each number has a terminal in the drawing. At both ends of every wire there is a ferrule carrying the wire number, and a metal sleeve that grips the strands of flexible wire.

A wire landed on -X2:5 instead of -X2:6, so the machine saw the wrong sensor. Read the drawing before you disconnect.

4Trace the wire to the PLC

Follow the signal: from the core, to the terminal, to the PLC input.
