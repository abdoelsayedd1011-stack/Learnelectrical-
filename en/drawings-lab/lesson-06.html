Reading drawings · Lesson 6 of 7

Panel layout and terminal strips

When you open the panel door you will find hundreds of wires. Two drawings let you find your way as if you already knew the panel: the panel layout (where each device is) and the terminal diagram (where each wire comes from and goes to). At the end you will trace signals from the field to the PLC yourself.

1The panel layout

A front view of the mounting plate, to scale. It shows every device with its tag, the ducts and the DIN rails, and sometimes a table of sizes and weights. It usually comes with a door drawing too (push buttons, lamps and the HMI).

Mounting plate =A1 +CP1

  • A logical arrangement: the power comes in at the top (main switch) and goes down: protection, contactors, and at the very bottom the terminals, next to the cable entry.
  • Keep power away from signals: the VFD and contactors as far as you can from the PLC and the sensor wiring.
  • Heat: devices that get hot (VFD, power supply) at the top, with the clearances the catalogue asks for.

2The terminals: -X1 and -X2

The terminals are the "border" between the panel and the field. Any cable from outside lands on a terminal first, and from the terminal a wire inside the panel goes to the device. That way, if you replace a sensor you do not touch the panel wiring.

ItemOn the drawing
Terminal stripA tag like -X1 for power and -X2 for control, and terminal -X2:6
Bridge (jumper)A line or dots joining terminals side by side (like +24V on 1, 2 and 3)
PE earth terminalGreen/yellow and connected to the DIN rail
Fuse / disconnect terminalA fuse or switch symbol on the terminal: it can be opened for testing

A terminal diagram is read from left to right: the field device → the cable and its cores → the terminal number → the wire inside the panel and its number → the device or the PLC input.

3Cable cores and ferrules

  • Colour-coded cores (IEC 60445): brown, black and grey for the phases, blue for the neutral, green/yellow for earth.
  • Sensor cables (M12): brown (BN) = +24V, blue (BU) = 0V, black (BK) = the signal, white (WH) = a second signal.
  • Numbered cores: in multicore control cables (like 7G1.5) the cores are printed 1, 2, 3... and the drawing tells you which number goes on which terminal.
  • Ferrule (wire marker): the marker on the end of the wire with its number. It must be on both ends, with the same number as on the drawing.
  • Wire end ferrule (metal sleeve): something different: it is crimped onto the end of a flexible wire so the strands do not spread in the terminal.
From the field: a sensor was replaced and a technician connected the signal wire to -X2:5 instead of -X2:6. Now the machine sees "the float switch" instead of "the limit switch". The solution: the drawing. Read the core and the terminal before you disconnect.

4Trace the wire to the PLC

You have 3 devices in the field. For each one, tap the core that carries the signal, then the terminal it lands on, then the PLC input.

From the field to the PLC

Safety: the -X1 terminals carry power-circuit voltage even when the contactor is off. And some terminals on -X2 may carry voltage fed from another panel. Treat every terminal as live until you have measured it.
