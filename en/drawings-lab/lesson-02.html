Symbols: IEC and ANSI/NEMA

Symbols are the alphabet of drawings. There are two schools: IEC (European, and most of our region) and ANSI/NEMA (American, which you will meet on American machines). In this lesson you will see both side by side, review them with cards, and practise until you can recognise any symbol at a glance.

The golden rule: a drawing is always drawn with the power off and no buttons pressed (normal state). NO means open in that state, and NC means closed. When the coil is energised, all its contacts change over.

3Things that confuse people

  • NO and NC in NEMA: the same two lines; the difference is the diagonal line. If it has a diagonal line across it, it is NC.
  • Push button or contact? In IEC the push button is the same contact with an actuator [ joined by a dashed line (mechanical link). Without the actuator it is a relay contact.
  • The overload has two symbols: the thermal element in the power circuit, on the phases, and the 95-96 contact in the control circuit. Both are the same device, -F2.
  • Timers: the parachute shape on a contact means its action is delayed. Check in the catalogue whether it is on-delay or off-delay.
  • Lamps: in NEMA, R red usually means "running" and G green "stopped". In IEC 60204, green means "running/safe" and red "danger/emergency". Check the legend page.
From the field: an American filling machine arrived with a European panel that had been modified. You will find half the drawings in NEMA and half in IEC. Do not get confused: read the name and the terminals, and the shape confirms it.
