Device tags, terminal numbers and cross-references

Symbols tell you what kind of device it is, but the numbers are what lead you to it in the panel. In this lesson you will learn why each device is named the way it is, why its terminals are numbered the way they are, and how to jump from a contact to its coil on another page with the cross-reference.

1What does a device name mean?

The name is a letter and a number. The letter says what the device does, and the number is its order in the drawing.

Switching and protection: -Q breaker and isolator, and -F fuse, MCB and overload. Control devices: -K relay, contactor and timer, -S button, selector and limit switch, -B sensor, and -H pilot lamp.

The rest: -M motor, -T transformer, -G power supply, -A PLC, -U VFD, -X terminal, and -W cable.

NEMA names differ: CB for a breaker, CR, M and TR for relay, contactor and timer, FU and OL, PB, SS and LS, and PL for a lamp. An IEC contactor may be -K1, -KM1 or -Q: read the symbols page.

2What do terminal numbers tell you?

Power terminals are 1-2, 3-4 and 5-6: odd is in and even is out. The coil is always A1 - A2. A lamp is X1 - X2.

Auxiliary contacts have a two-digit number: the first digit is the order of the contact and the second is its type. Ending in 3-4 means NO (such as 13-14 and 43-44), and ending in 1-2 means NC (such as 21-22 and 31-32). So 33-34 is the third contact and it is NO.

The overload: 95-96 is NC and goes in the coil path, and 97-98 is NO for the fault lamp. The timer: 15-16 opens and 15-18 closes.

-K1:13 means terminal 13 on K1, and -X2:6 means terminal 6 on strip X2.

3How are wires numbered?

Every electrical node has one number, printed on a ferrule at both ends of the wire. As soon as the wire passes through a device, the number changes.

There are 3 numbering schemes. Sequential (1, 2, 3) is simple and common. Page.column.wire such as 3.4.1 takes you to its place in the drawing. By destination such as -K1:A1 tells you where the wire goes.

Same wire number = same voltage. If you find 230 V at one end and 0 V at the other, the wire is broken.

4Cross-reference: the coil on one page, its contacts on another

A coil and its contacts are not drawn side by side, so the drawing links them with a reference. /3.4 means page 3, column 4.

Under each contact you find where its coil is. Under each coil you find a table of all its contacts and where each one is. Try it: tap any blue reference.

Two pages and cross-references

In NEMA: rung numbers are on the left, and next to the coil are the rung numbers of its contacts (NC is underlined).
