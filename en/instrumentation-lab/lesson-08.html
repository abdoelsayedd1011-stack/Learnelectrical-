Load cells and scales

Every scale in a plant, from filling scales to weighbridges and tank scales, has a load cell inside: a piece of metal that stretches a hair's breadth when loaded, with sensors bonded to it that feel that stretch. Here you will load a cell yourself and watch the bridge work, calibrate an indicator step by step like a technician, and pick up the multimeter to find the faulty cell.

1How does a load cell sense weight?

Inside a load cell is a strain gauge: a fine wire bonded to a metal body. When the load stretches the body the wire lengthens and its resistance rises, and when it compresses the body the resistance falls.

But the change is tiny: a 350 Ω cell at full load changes by only 0.7 Ω. That is why we need a bridge to magnify the difference.

2The bridge and mV/V

The cell has 4 gauges wired as a bridge. A voltage called excitation (5 or 10 V) goes in, and a signal in millivolts comes out, linear with the load:

mV = mV/V × V exc × load ÷ capacity

Example: a 500 kg cell with 2 mV/V sensitivity on 10 V:

  • 500 kg
    20 mV
  • 250 kg
    10 mV
  • 1 kg
    0.04 mV

So one kilogram = 40 microvolts. Noise is the enemy of weighing.

3What types of cell are there?

S-type is for suspension: a hanging hopper and tension tests. Shear beam is for floor scales and tanks. Single point is for a scale with one cell, such as filling and checkout scales. Canister is for weighbridges and silos and carries tonnes.

44-wire or 6-wire?

A 4-wire cell has 2 wires for excitation and 2 for signal. Its drawback is that when the cable temperature changes, the reading drifts.

A 6-wire cell adds two sense wires that measure the voltage actually arriving at the cell, so the indicator compensates.

5Several cells together

A tank on 4 legs needs 4 cells. They are wired in parallel in a summing box, so the indicator sees them as one cell with 4 times the capacity.

After wiring comes corner adjustment: the same weight on every corner must give the same reading. The box should be IP67 with silica gel inside, because moisture is the most common cause of drift. The indicator outputs the weight as 4–20 mA, Profinet or relays.

6Calibration: zero and span

5 steps: enter the capacity and division. With the scale empty, do ZERO. Put on a certified weight of 50 to 100 % of capacity and do SPAN. Check and try the corners. Record.

Keep two words apart. ZERO is part of calibration: it fixes the zero point and is stored. TARE is for daily work: it subtracts the empty container temporarily.

Calibrate the indicator

If you span with a "50 kg" sack that really weighs 49.3, the error is 1.4 % (140 kg in 10 tonnes). Certified weights only.

7How do you check a cell with a multimeter?

Between +EXC and −EXC it should read 350–420 Ω (usually 390). Between +SIG and −SIG about 350 Ω. From +EXC to each signal wire the two readings must be equal, and a difference means the cell is deformed.

Between any wire and the body it must read OL, and any value means moisture. An unloaded cell should give under ±0.2 mV on 10 V (zero balance).

A 50 V megger only, 500 V kills the gauges. And before any welding on the frame: disconnect the cells.

8Each symptom and its cause

Zero drifts: moisture in the junction box or cable. One corner reads different: corner adjustment, or a weak cell. Reads less than the truth: something is carrying part of the weight, such as a rigid pipe, a ladder or built-up dust.

Side load: the load is off-axis, fit the right mounting kit. Failed after welding: welding current burnt the cell. Zero shifted after a knock: permanent overload.

The indicator is on 220 V. And never go under a suspended tank while it is loaded.
