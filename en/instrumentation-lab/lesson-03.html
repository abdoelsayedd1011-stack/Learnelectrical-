Digital sensors

Not every sensor measures a value. Most sensors on machines say just one thing: something there or not. Has the box arrived? Is the door closed? Has the cylinder finished its stroke? Here you will learn the types, finally understand PNP and NPN, and wire them yourself to a PLC card.

1What kinds of digital sensors are there?

4 main kinds. A limit switch is mechanical and must be struck by the object. An inductive sensor senses metal only, from 2 to 15 mm. A capacitive sensor senses anything: plastic, powder, water. A photoelectric sensor works with a light beam and reaches metres.

Limit switchmechanical, gets hit
Inductivemetal only · 2–15 mm
Capacitiveanything: plastic, powder, water
Photoelectrica light beam · up to metres

The photoelectric sensor itself comes in 3 types. Through-beam: an emitter and receiver facing each other, up to 20 m, the most reliable. Retro-reflective: a reflector opposite, up to 10 m, and for shiny objects we choose a polarized type. Diffuse: the beam bounces off the object itself, so the range is short and black is harder.

The sensing distance Sn in the datasheet is for steel. Stainless and aluminium are less (×0.7 or ×0.4). A flush type reaches less than a non-flush one.

2NO or NC?

An NO sensor has its output OFF until it senses something (on a photoelectric sensor this is light-on). An NC sensor has its output ON until it senses (dark-on).

Anything that stops the machine must be NC: if the wire breaks, the machine stops.

3PNP or NPN?

The difference is what the output delivers. PNP outputs +24 V (sourcing), so the COM of the PLC card sits on 0 V. This is the most common here.

NPN pulls the output to 0 V (sinking), so the COM sits on +24 V. You find it on Japanese and Chinese equipment.

The colours of a 3-wire sensor: brown BN is +24 V, blue BU is 0 V, and black BK is the output going to the PLC. A white WH wire is a second output.

Do not trust the card name. See where the COM goes: on 0 V it needs PNP, on +24 V it needs NPN.

5How is a 2-wire sensor different?

A 2-wire sensor takes its own power from the same circuit. So when OFF it leaks a small current (0.5–1.5 mA), and when ON it drops 5–8 V.

If the input LED glows dimly all the time, that is the leakage. Fit a bleeder resistor in parallel with the input, or change to a 3-wire sensor.

6Each symptom and its cause

Sensor LED on but the PLC does not see it: an NPN sensor in place of a PNP, check the label. Sensor LED blinking: a short on the output or too much load. Sees it sometimes and not others: the object is at the edge of the range, mount it at 70–80 % of Sn.

Shiny cans pass unseen: use a polarized retro-reflective sensor. A signal with nothing in front: dust on the lens or swarf on the proximity sensor.

A sensor on a guard door or a light curtain = a safety circuit. Bypassing is forbidden.
