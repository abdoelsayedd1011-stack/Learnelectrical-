Pressure, level and flow

These three variables are the daily work of any plant handling liquids: water, steam, oil, milk, chemicals. You will learn the types of pressure transmitter, how a simple pressure reading at the bottom of a tank tells you the level (and when it lies to you), and how to choose the right flowmeter without wasting the plant's money.

1Pressure: gauge, absolute and DP

TypeMeasured relative toExample
Gauge (g)Atmospheric pressure: reads zero in open airPump, compressor, hydraulics
Absolute (a)Perfect vacuum: reads about 1.013 bar in open airVacuum, distillation, saturated steam pressure
Differential (DP)The difference between two ports, H and LA clogging filter, orifice flow, closed tank level
Units: 1 bar = 100 kPa ≈ 14.5 psi ≈ 10.2 m water ≈ 1.02 kg/cm² and 1 mbar = 100 Pa. Check the range and unit on the transmitter nameplate before you program the PLC.

Manifold: a valve block between the pipe and the transmitter so you can isolate and calibrate it without stopping the line. On a DP it has 3 or 5 valves: one valve for each side (H and L) and an equalizing valve between them.

  1. To take a DP out of service: open the equalizing valve first (so one side never sees the full line pressure on its own), then close H and L.
  2. To put it back: open H, then close the equalizing valve, then open L.
Safety: never undo a pressure transmitter while it is under pressure. Close the isolation valve, release the pressure through the vent valve, and check the gauge reads zero. 10 bar will blow the transmitter into your face, and steam and chemicals are worse. Any transmitter in an Ex area must follow that area's permit to work.

2Level by pressure: h = P ÷ ρg

A column of liquid creates pressure at the bottom according to its height and density. So if you put a pressure transmitter at the bottom of the tank, you can calculate the level:

P = ρ × g × h ⇒ h = P ÷ (ρ × g)
Example: a water tank (ρ = 1000) and the transmitter measures 29.4 kPa ⇒ h = 29400 ÷ (1000 × 9.81) ≈ 3 m. The same pressure in a diesel tank (ρ = 840) means ≈ 3.57 m! So the transmitter must know the density.

Q = k × √ΔP
The square root: with an orifice the flow is proportional to the square root of the pressure difference. At half the flow range, the DP is only a quarter of its range (0.5² = 0.25). That is why you must enable square root extraction in the transmitter or in the PLC, not both! If it is done twice or forgotten entirely, the reading is wrong at every point except zero and 100 %.
A magmeter must run full: the pipe must always be full at a magmeter (fit it on a rising line or at a low point), and the liquid must be grounded with grounding rings or a ground electrode, especially in plastic pipes.

6Common field faults

Common field faults:
  • A pressure transmitter reads a fixed value and doesn't move: the isolation valve is closed, or the impulse line is blocked or frozen in winter.
  • A DP level transmitter reads wrong after start-up: the low-side line has condensate in it (wet leg) or is empty, depending on the design, or someone left the equalizing valve open.
  • A magmeter shows flow with the pump stopped: the pipe is not full or the grounding is poor.
  • Orifice flow is wrong mid-range: square root done twice or not at all.
  • Pressure-based level reads high in a closed tank: it is measuring the gas pressure on top. Use DP.
