Pressure, level and flow

These three variables are the daily work of any plant handling liquids: water, steam, oil, milk, chemicals. You will learn the types of pressure transmitter, how a simple pressure reading at the bottom of a tank tells you the level (and when it lies to you), and how to choose the right flowmeter without wasting the plant's money.

1How many ways is pressure measured?

3 ways, depending on what you compare with. Gauge (bar g) treats atmospheric pressure as zero, used for pumps and hydraulics. Absolute (bar a) measures from a perfect vacuum, so air is 1.013, used for vacuum and steam. DP measures the difference between two points H and L, used for filters, flow and closed tanks.

1 bar = 100 kPa ≈ 14.5 psi ≈ 10.2 m of water

A DP transmitter has 3 valves, and their order matters. To take it out of service: open the equalizing valve, then close H and L. To put it back: open H, close the equalizing valve, then open L.

Never undo a transmitter under pressure: close the isolation, vent, and make sure the gauge reads zero.

2How do you measure level by pressure?

A liquid creates pressure at the bottom of a tank in proportion to its height and density. So measure the pressure and you know the level:

h = P ÷ (ρ × g)

But density matters. The same 29.4 kPa means 3 m for water (ρ 1000) and 3.57 m for diesel (ρ 840). So the transmitter must know the density.

3Are there other ways to measure level?

Radar is unaffected by density or vapour and is the most common. Ultrasonic is cheaper, but foam and vapour confuse it. A float is simple but sticks.

Capacitive is for liquids and powders and needs calibrating on the product. A switch (fork) gives ON/OFF only, to protect against overflow and dry running. A load cell is the most accurate for batching.

4What measures flow?

Magnetic is for water and juices, and does not work with oil, diesel or gas. The pipe must be always full and the liquid earthed. Vortex is for steam and gas, not for low velocities. Turbine is for clean liquids and fuel.

Coriolis measures mass and density precisely but is expensive. An orifice with DP is for steam and large sizes and is cheap. Its flow is worked out from the square root of the differential pressure:

Q = k × √ΔP

The square root is done in the transmitter or the PLC, not both. And remember: half the flow = a quarter of the DP.

6Each wrong reading and its cause

Pressure stuck and not moving: the isolation valve is closed, or the line is blocked or frozen. Wrong DP level: a wet leg problem or the equalizing valve is open. Level too high in a closed tank: the transmitter is measuring gas pressure too, use DP.

Magnetic meter reads with the pump stopped: the pipe is not full or the earthing is wrong. Orifice wrong at mid-range: the square root is applied twice or not at all.
