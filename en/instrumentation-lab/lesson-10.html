1The loop check

Before any new plant starts up (or after a modification), every loop is checked from the sensor to the HMI screen: that the wiring goes to the right place, the range is right, and the alarms work.

  1. Visual inspection: the installed device matches the tag, model and range in the datasheet, the glands are tight, the shield is grounded correctly.
  2. Wiring check: continuity of every wire, and insulation between wires and earth (with an insulation tester, with the devices disconnected).
  3. Signal injection: with the calibrator apply 0, 50 and 100 % (real pressure or simulate) and check that the PLC and HMI show the same value.
  4. Alarms and interlocks: reach the high and low points and check that the alarm appears and the interlock operates.
  5. For valves: send 0, 25, 50, 75 and 100 % and check the valve opens correctly and the feedback signal comes back.
  6. Record and sign: a loop check sheet signed by instrumentation and operations.

2The calibrator: source, measure and simulate

ModeWhat it doesWhen you use it
MeasureMeasures mA (and often supplies 24V to the loop)Reading the transmitter output while you calibrate it
SourceDrives the mA itself (active)Testing a PLC card or a valve: "send 12 mA and check the PLC says 50 %"
SimulateActs like a 2-wire transmitter (passive), powered by the loopDisconnect the transmitter, put the calibrator in its place and control the current

There are also pressure calibrators (with a hand pump), temperature calibrators (a dry block, or simulating thermocouple mV and PT100 Ω), and test weights for scales. The calibrator itself must have a valid calibration certificate, and be at least 4 times more accurate than the device you are calibrating (TUR 4:1).

35-point calibration

We apply 0, 25, 50, 75 and 100 % of range (sometimes going up and down to check hysteresis), and calculate the error as a percentage of span:

Error % = (measured mA − expected mA) ÷ 16 × 100
  • As-found: the reading as you found it, before any adjustment. Very important: it tells quality whether the product made in the previous period was within spec.
  • As-left: the reading after adjustment. It must be within tolerance.
  • Adjustment: zero first (at LRV), then span (at URV), then go back and check the zero, because on some devices the two interact.

4How to think about any fault

  1. Believe the operator, but verify: compare the reading with something independent (a gauge, a thermometer, a sight glass).
  2. Split the loop in two: measure the mA. If the current is right and the PLC is wrong ⇒ the problem is in the card or the scaling. If the current is wrong ⇒ it is in the transmitter, the sensor or the process.
  3. Read the number: 0 mA = open loop. Above 21 = a short or a fail. Steady while the process changes = isolation valve / blocked line / weak supply. Unstable = noise.
  4. Ask what has changed: maintenance yesterday? Rain? A new VFD? Welding?
  5. Fix and record the cause, not just the symptom.
