Analog signals: 4–20 mA and 0–10 V

Most transmitters in the world speak one language: a current from 4 to 20 milliamps. In this lesson you will understand why current and not voltage, why it starts at 4 and not zero, how to convert mA to a value, how to measure the loop properly, and how to tell the fault from the meter reading.

2Why 4, not zero?

💡Live zero: the lowest value is 4 mA, so well below it = a fault. The 4 mA also powers the transmitter.
  • < 3.6 mAfault: open wire or fail low
  • 3.8 – 4 mAslightly under range, healthy
  • 4 – 20 mAnormal measurement
  • 20 – 20.5 mAslightly over range, healthy
  • > 21 mAfault: short or fail high

7Measuring loop current right

  1. Loop to Manual
  2. Meter on mA DC, red in the mA jack
  3. Open the loop, meter in series
  4. Read and compare
  5. Reconnect, back to Auto

Meter on mA in parallel = a short and a wrong reading. In parallel you measure volts only.
