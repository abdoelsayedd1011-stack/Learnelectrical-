Instrumentation · Lesson 1 of 10

What is instrumentation?

Every machine in a plant needs to sense (temperature, pressure, weight, speed), then decide, then act. Instrumentation is the part that senses and passes that information to the PLC. In this lesson you will understand the whole loop, run a real heating loop yourself, and learn to read the tags on drawings and devices.

1What is instrumentation for in a plant?

A plant is like the human body. The sensor is the hand that feels, the controller is the brain that decides, and the valve is the other hand that acts.

So instrumentation has two jobs: measurement and process control. If the reading is wrong, the whole plant runs wrong.

2What is a control loop made of?

Every control loop is 4 parts in a row. The sensor (such as a PT100 or thermocouple) feels the value. The transmitter turns it into a 4–20 mA signal. The controller (PLC or E5CC) compares and decides. The actuator (valve, VFD or SSR) acts.

The controller works with 4 numbers. PV is the reading now (say 63 °C). SP is the value you want (70 °C). Error is the gap between them (7 °C). OUT is the controller's command to the actuator (valve at 65 %).

In Auto the controller moves the valve and the loop is closed. In Manual you set the opening yourself, which we use during maintenance.

Temperature control loop

If PV sits at 60 with the valve 100 % open, the problem is not the controller: weak steam, a valve that does not open, or a sensor reading wrong. Compare with a hand thermometer first.

4What is a range?

Every transmitter has two limits. LRV is the lowest value it measures and gives 4 mA, and URV is the highest and gives 20 mA. The distance between them is the span:

Span = URV − LRV

For example, a transmitter ranged 0 to 150 °C:

  • 0 °C
    4 mA
  • 75 °C
    12 mA
  • 150 °C
    20 mA

With a range of −50 to 150 °C, 4 mA means −50 °C, not zero.

Watch two words. Accuracy is how close the reading is to the truth, such as ±0.1 % of span. Resolution is the smallest step the display shows. So the screen can say 72.48 while the truth is 75.

Pick the range to fit the job: a 0–100 bar range while you work at 3 bar = an error that can reach 3 %.

5What does the tag TT-101 mean?

Every device in the plant has a tag. The first letter says what it measures, the letters after it give its function, and the number is the loop number.

TT
101TIC
101PDT
205LSH
310

First letter: T temperature, P pressure, F flow, L level, W weight, S speed, Z position.

Following letters: T transmitter, E element, I indicator, C controller, S switch, V valve, A alarm, H high, L low, D differential.

Devices in the same loop share the number: TE-101, TT-101, TIC-101 and TV-101.

6Problems from the field

If someone says "the PID is not working", the controller was most likely left in Manual. If the reading is off by a constant ratio, the transmitter range differs from the range entered in the PLC. If the tag on the device differs from the drawing, update the drawings (as-built).

Before disconnecting a transmitter: tell operations and put the loop in Manual so the valve does not drive fully open.
