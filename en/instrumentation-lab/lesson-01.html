What is instrumentation?

Every machine in a plant needs to sense (temperature, pressure, weight, speed), then decide, then act. Instrumentation is the part that senses and passes that information to the PLC. In this lesson you will understand the whole loop, run a real heating loop yourself, and learn to read the tags on drawings and devices.

1A plant is like the human body

Think of it like this: the sensor is like your hand feeling that the water is hot, the PLC or controller is like the brain deciding "that's too hot", and the valve or motor is like your other hand turning off the tap. Without the sensing, the brain can't do anything.

Key point: instrumentation = measurement devices and process control devices. Any error in a reading and the whole plant acts wrongly while believing it is right.

2The control loop: 4 parts

  1. Sensor / element: the part that actually senses the variable, like a thermocouple, a PT100 or a strain gauge. Its output is a weak signal (millivolts or ohms).
  2. Transmitter: takes the weak sensor signal and turns it into a strong standard signal that survives long distances: usually 4–20 mA.
  3. Controller: a PLC or a temperature controller like the E5CC. It compares the reading with what you want and sends out a command.
  4. Actuator / final element: the part that acts: a control valve, a VFD changing a pump's speed, or a heater on an SSR.

Auto and Manual: in Auto the controller moves the valve according to the error (closed loop). In Manual you set the opening yourself and the controller just watches (open loop). In the field we switch to Manual during maintenance or when the sensor has failed.

3Try it yourself: a tank heating loop

This is a water tank heated by steam. TT-101 measures the temperature and sends 4–20 mA to the controller TIC-101, and the controller opens and closes the steam valve TV-101. Move the SP, try Manual, and open the cold water to see how the controller reacts. (Time here runs 8 times faster than real life.)

Temperature control loop

From the field: a CIP tank in a dairy, SP 75 °C. If the PV stays at 60 with the valve 100 % open, the problem is not the controller: either the steam is weak, or the valve is not really opening (the actuator air is cut off), or the sensor reads wrong. Always compare the reading with a hand thermometer before you touch the PID settings.

4Range: LRV, URV and span

Every transmitter is set to a range: the lowest value (LRV, Lower Range Value) gives 4 mA, and the highest value (URV, Upper Range Value) gives 20 mA. The span = URV − LRV.

Span = URV − LRV
Example: TT-101 has a range of 0–150 °C ⇒ the span is 150 °C. At 75 °C (mid-range) the output = 12 mA. If its range is −50 to 150 °C ⇒ the span is 200 °C, and 4 mA means −50 °C, not zero!
TermMeaningExample
AccuracyHow close the reading is to the truth±0.1 % of span ⇒ on a 0–150 °C range that is ±0.15 °C
ResolutionThe smallest change the device can showThe display shows 0.1 °C
RepeatabilityGives the same reading when measuring the same thing several times±0.05 %
Don't be fooled: a display showing two decimal places (high resolution) does not mean the device is accurate. It can show 72.48 °C when the truth is 75 °C. Resolution is not accuracy.

An important rule: choose a range that fits the job. A pressure transmitter with a 0–100 bar range working at 3 bar has an accuracy of ±0.1 % of 100 = ±0.1 bar, so the error could be 3 % of your reading!

5Tags: what does TT-101 mean?

6From the field

Common field faults:
  • The controller was left in Manual after the last maintenance, and people say "the PID isn't working". Check the mode first.
  • The range in the transmitter differs from the range in the PLC: the transmitter is 0–10 bar and the PLC is programmed for 0–16 bar ⇒ the reading is off by a fixed ratio.
  • The tag on the device differs from the drawing after a modification: record every change on the drawings (as-built) so the next person doesn't get lost.
Safety: 24V DC signals are not dangerous to you, but the loop may be holding a valve or a heater. Before you disconnect any transmitter, inform operations and put the loop in Manual so the controller doesn't go wild and drive the valve fully open or shut. In hazardous areas (Ex) there are special rules for opening devices.
