Power and energy

How much does this appliance "eat"? And why does the water heater push the bill up while a lamp doesn’t? We will understand the watt and the kilowatt-hour, work out the bill for a whole home, and see why a wire heats up and a fuse blows.

1What is power?

Power is how fast energy is used, measured in watts W: 1 watt is 1 joule every second. It is worked out from voltage and current:

P = V × I

If you do not know one of them, use the resistance: with current and resistance it is P = I² × R, and with voltage and resistance it is P = V² ÷ R.

Because voltage is squared: halve the voltage and power falls to a quarter. A 2000 W heater on 110 V gives only 500 W.

3Watts, kilowatts and horsepower

The watt is a small unit: an LED lamp is about 10 W. For bigger appliances we use the kilowatt (kW = 1000 W), such as a 2 kW heater, and for power stations the megawatt (MW = a million watts).

Motors are also rated in horsepower (HP ≈ 746 W). So a 7.5 kW motor is 10 HP.

A "1.5 HP air conditioner" is a cooling size. The real power is on the sticker.

4How is the electricity bill worked out?

The bill is not for power but for energy: power multiplied by time. The meter counts it in kWh, and 1 kWh is 1000 watts running for an hour.

kWh = (W ÷ 1000) × h

An ordinary home uses about 15 kWh a day, split like this:

  • 💡 6 LED bulbs
    0.36 kWh
  • 🧊 Fridge
    1.5 kWh
  • 🚿 Heater 1 h
    2 kWh
  • ❄️ AC 8 h
    11.2 kWh

So the air conditioner alone is about two thirds of the bill.

5Why does a wire get hot?

Any current through a resistance gives off heat equal to P = I² × R. Because current is squared, double the current gives 4 times the heat. The same wire at 9 A gives off 23 W, which is normal, and at 30 A it gives off 250 W, which is a wire on fire.

Sometimes that heat is wanted: a heater is built to get hot, and a fuse is built to melt and break the circuit if the current rises. Sometimes it is a danger: a loose screw in a socket heats up and blackens it, and a coiled extension reel traps the heat and melts.

6What is efficiency?

No device turns all its electricity into useful work. Part is lost as heat. Efficiency is the ratio of what comes out to what goes in:

η = Pout ÷ Pin × 100%
  • 💡 Filament bulb
    5%
  • 💡 LED bulb
    35%
  • 🌀 Small motor
    50–70%
  • 🏭 Large motor
    85–95%

A motor delivering 1.5 kW at 85 % efficiency draws 1.76 kW from the supply.
