Measuring appliances in practice

This is the real work: an appliance comes into the workshop, and you find out whether it is good or faulty using only a multimeter. You will test the water heater, the kettle, the lamp, the fuse and the motor, and then we hide a fault for you to find.

1The rule before you start

Every measurement in this lesson is in ohms, so the appliance must be completely disconnected from the supply (the plug out of the socket, or the breaker off and checked). The only exception: the clamp meter at the end of the lesson, which measures current with the appliance running.

How do you know the right reading? For any appliance that heats (water heater, kettle, iron, lamp) the resistance can be worked out from the ratings written on it:

R = V² ÷ P
Example: a 2000W water heater on 220V ⇒ R = 220 × 220 ÷ 2000 = 24.2Ω. If you read about that, the element is good. If you read OL, the element is open (burnt out).

And there is another very important measurement: between any live terminal and the appliance’s metal body. It must read OL. If it reads a number, there is leakage and the body can give a shock to whoever touches it.

3The motor: important notes

Three-phase motor: it has 3 identical windings: U1-U2, V1-V2 and W1-W2. Remove the links (star or delta) from the terminal box first so you can measure each winding on its own.
• The three windings must read about the same (less than 5% difference).
• Between two different windings: OL.
• Between any winding and the body: OL.
Single-phase motor (fan, washing machine, pump): it has a Run winding (lower resistance) and a Start winding (higher resistance), with a Common point.
• The highest reading is between S and R, and equals the two added together: S-R = C-S + C-R.
• The terminal not involved in the highest reading is C.
• From C: the smaller reading goes to R, the larger to S.
A multimeter is not enough for insulation: a multimeter measures with a small voltage (about 3V), so it can read OL even when the insulation is weak. Technicians use an insulation tester (megger) that measures at 500V or 1000V, and an acceptable reading for a motor is usually above 1MΩ. Never run a motor that has been under water without testing it with a megger.

4Measuring current with a clamp meter

A clamp meter goes around the wire from the outside and measures the current from its magnetic field, with the appliance running, without cutting anything. The only thing to remember: one wire only inside the jaws.

The clamp meter

Why measure current? A 2000W water heater should draw about 9A. If you read 4.5A, half the element is faulty (in heaters with two elements). And if a motor draws more than its nameplate says, it is overloaded or has a problem.

To measure without stripping the cable, there is a "line splitter" that plugs in between the socket and the appliance and separates the live from the neutral.
