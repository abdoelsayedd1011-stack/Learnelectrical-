Types of electricity

There is direct current, DC, like a battery, and alternating current, AC, like the mains in your home. And the mains itself is either single phase or three phase. At the end you will learn the wire colours so you can identify each wire without guessing.

1DC and AC: the difference in one sentence

DC (direct current) ⎓
The current flows in one direction and the voltage is steady. It has a positive + terminal and a negative − terminal.
Examples: a 1.5V remote battery, a 9V battery, a 12V car battery, a 5V phone charger output, a 24V control-panel power supply.
AC (alternating current) ~
The current reverses direction, back and forth. In Egypt it reverses 50 times a second (50 hertz, Hz).
Examples: the 220V socket at home, the 380V supply coming into factories.

Why is the house supply AC? Because AC is easy to step up and down with a transformer, so it can travel long distances from the power station to your home without big losses. You will see the transformer in lesson 10.

Electricity on the screen

Why does the meter say 220 when the peak is 311? With AC the voltage changes every instant, so we use one number called the effective value (RMS): 220V AC heats a heater exactly as much as 220V DC. And the peak = 220 × 1.414 = 311V. A multimeter on V~ shows you the 220.

3Single phase and three phase

Single phase 220V (homes)

3 wires: the live L, which carries the supply, the neutral N, which is the return path, and the earth PE, for protection only. Between live and neutral there are 220V.

Three phase 380V (factories and buildings)

3 phases L1 L2 L3 + neutral + earth. Between any two phases there are 380V, and between any phase and neutral 220V. It runs big motors and machines.

A practical example: an apartment building gets a three-phase supply from the utility, and each flat takes one phase + neutral. That is why your flat can be without power while the one next door still has it: each is on a different phase.

5Why is earthing so important?

If a live wire inside a water heater or washing machine loses its insulation and touches the metal body, the whole body has 220V on it. Whoever touches it, the current flows through their body to earth.

In short: the earth doesn’t run anything; it is a safety path. Any appliance with a metal body (washing machine, water heater, fridge, oven) must be connected to earth, and the socket must have a real earth connected to an earthing system.
