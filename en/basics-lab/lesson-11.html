Ohm’s law, power and choosing the cable and breaker

From the power written on an appliance you will work out the current, and from that choose the right breaker and a cable size that can carry it without heating up.

‹ Lessons← All courses

1A quick reminder

We covered power and energy in detail in lesson 5, and voltage and current in lesson 2. Here we only need two formulas to choose the cable and the breaker:

I = P ÷ V
Current from power. The power is written on the appliance and the voltage is 220V. This is the one you will use every day.
V = I × R
Ohm’s law. If you know two, you can find the third: I = V ÷ R and R = V ÷ I.

3A full example: a 3000W water heater

  1. Current: I = 3000 ÷ 220 = 13.6A.
  2. Breaker: the smallest standard breaker above 13.6A ⇒ 16A.
  3. Cable: it must carry the breaker’s current (16A) ⇒ 2.5mm² copper. (And if the run is long, check the voltage drop with the calculator in lesson 3.)
Why is the cable sized to the breaker and not to the appliance? The breaker is what protects the cable. If you put a 25A breaker on a 1.5mm² cable, the cable will heat up and melt its insulation before the breaker notices anything, and that causes many fires.
A practical note: if the cable is long (far from the panel) or the load runs for long hours continuously, like a water heater, many electricians go one step up: 4mm² and a 20A breaker.

4The usual working table

Cable size (copper)Maximum breakerUsed for
1.5 mm²10ALighting
2.5 mm²16AOrdinary sockets, a water heater up to 3000W
4 mm²20–25AWater heater, air conditioner, oven
6 mm²32AElectric cooker, a feeder
10 mm²40AThe main supply to a flat
This is the usual working table for PVC-insulated copper wires in conduit in the wall. The official values change with the cable length, the installation method, the number of wires in the conduit and the temperature. Always check your local code, and an engineer if the load is large.

Standard breakers: 6, 10, 16, 20, 25, 32, 40A. Home breakers are curve B or C (C tolerates the high starting current of motors and air conditioners).
