The multimeter (AVO)

The multimeter is the eye you see electricity with. The old name AVO comes from Ampere, Volt and Ohm. Here you hold it in your own hands: turn the dial, place the probes and read, and you can even try the dangerous mistakes safely.

The jacks: the black lead always goes in COM. The red goes in VΩ for almost everything, and only moves to 10A when you measure amps in series.

The two most dangerous mistakes: (1) measuring volts with the red lead still in the 10A jack ⇒ a short circuit and sparks. (2) measuring ohms or continuity on something live ⇒ the meter burns out. Look at the dial and the jack before you touch anything.

2How to use it here

  1. Tap the mode you want around the rotary dial (V~, V⎓, Ω ...).
  2. Tap the first point for the red probe, and the second for the black. (Or pick the probe with the buttons.)
  3. Read the display. To move the red lead to the 10A jack, tap the jack itself.
Try the mistakes safely: nothing burns here. Put the red lead in 10A and measure the socket, or turn to Ω with the probes on the socket, and see what the meter tells you.

4How to read the number

  • OL (Over Limit / Open Line): the resistance is very high or the circuit is open, meaning no continuity.
  • A minus sign before the number on V⎓: the probes are reversed. The value is right, but red is on the negative.
  • kΩ means a thousand ohms, and MΩ a million ohms.
  • Before measuring ohms, touch the two probes together: it should read about 0.0 to 0.3Ω. That is the resistance of the probe leads themselves.
An example from work: you get a call that "the socket is dead". You measure between L and N: if it is 0 and the tester doesn’t light ⇒ the live isn’t reaching it (check the breaker). If there are 226V between L and PE but zero between L and N ⇒ the neutral is broken. You will play this game in lesson 13.
